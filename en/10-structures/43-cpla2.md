# Molecular Structure Deep Dive 43: cPLA2α (PLA2G4A) — The "Master Valve of the Arachidonic Acid Economy" and the Membrane Phospholipid Faucet

> **Series Position**: 10-structures (Molecular Structure Deep Dive), Article 43 — Dissecting the three-dimensional structure of cytosolic phospholipase A2α (cPLA2α/PLA2G4A), understanding "how cPLA2α cleaves arachidonic acid (AA) off membrane phospholipids," "how calcium ions activate it," "how the C2 domain anchors it to the membrane," and its "master valve" role in the "AA economy—inflammation—aging." LOX ([40-lox.md](40-lox.md)) converts AA to leukotrienes, COX-2 ([42-cox2.md](42-cox2.md)) converts AA to prostaglandins — and cPLA2α is the "source faucet of AA": it releases AA first, and only then can the two downstream pathways burn.

## 1. Why It Matters: cPLA2α Is the "Master Valve" of the "AA Economy"

cPLA2α (PLA2G4A) is the rate-limiting enzyme for "membrane phospholipid → AA" — the "core 'valve' at the 'source' of lipid inflammation":

- **"'Master switch' for 'AA release'"**: cPLA2α hydrolyzes membrane phospholipid sn-2 position → releases AA (+ lysophospholipid) → AA is the "fuel" for LOX/COX — "master switch 'on' → fuel 'AA' flows out (from membrane) → LOX/COX 'have material' to burn — the 'source' valve";
- **"'Upstream' of 'three pathways'"**: AA → LOX (leukotrienes, [40-lox.md](40-lox.md)) / COX (prostaglandins, [42-cox2.md](42-cox2.md)) / CYP (EET) → cPLA2α is the "common upstream" — "one 'faucet', three 'pipes' (LOX/COX/CYP) — 'master valve' controls 'three pathways' — 'upstream' leverage";
- **"'Calcium' 'switch'"**: cPLA2α is a "calcium-dependent" enzyme (μM-level Ca²⁺) → cellular stimulation (receptor/calcium signaling) → Ca²⁺↑ → C2 domain binds membrane + catalytic domain cleaves AA — "calcium 'turns' the valve (Ca²⁺↑ → enzyme 'goes to membrane') — 'signal' drives 'release'";
- **"'Master engine' of 'inflammation'"**: cPLA2α releases AA → prostaglandins/leukotrienes → inflammation → cPLA2α inhibitors are "anti-inflammatory" — "master valve 'controls' inflammation 'fuel' → close valve 'cut supply' (cPLA2α inhibitor) → inflammation 'drops' — 'upstream' anti-inflammatory target";
- **"'Link' to 'aging'"**: cPLA2α/AA pathway increases with age/chronic inflammation → lipid mediators↑ → inflammaging — "master valve 'constantly open' (chronic inflammation) → fuel 'flows continuously' (AA↑) → 'inflammaging' — 'source' imbalance";

**"'Connection to knowledge base' II"**: cPLA2α shares "membrane lipid metabolism" with "ferroptosis" ([39-gpx4.md](39-gpx4.md)/[40-lox.md](40-lox.md)) — "AA release → esterification (ACSL4) → oxidation (LOX) → ferroptosis vs. inflammation (COX) — 'two faces' of 'lipid fire' (ferroptosis/inflammation) — cPLA2α is the 'common' source — 'anti-aging' requires 'dual-face' management (anti-inflammatory + anti-ferroptosis) — 'master valve' perspective 'integrates'".

**Structure Overview (1CJY/1RLW, human cPLA2α catalytic domain + C2 domain)**: cPLA2α contains "N-terminal C2 domain (calcium-phospholipid binding) + C-terminal catalytic domain (α/β hydrolase fold)" — "dual-domain 'division of labor': C2 'senses calcium + anchors membrane' (1RLW), catalytic domain 'cuts lipid' (1CJY) — active site 'serine' (Ser228 catalytic triad) — 'hydrophobic channel' extends toward membrane — the 'scissor' structure that 'cuts' AA is 'clear'".

## 2. Structural Anatomy: What the "Master Valve" Looks Like in 1CJY/1RLW

### 2.0 Atom by Atom: How cPLA2α "Cuts Lipid"

- **"'Precision' of 'sn-2'"**: Phospholipid sn-2 position (glycerol second hydroxyl) ester bond → cPLA2α hydrolyzes here → releases AA (if sn-2 is AA) — "scissors 'precisely' cut sn-2 (glycerol position 2) — 'AA enriched' (sn-2 is often AA) — 'cut the right' position";
- **"'Catalytic triad'"**: Ser228-Asp549-Arg200 (or Ser/Asp dyad) → serine "attacks" the ester bond → hydrolysis — "Ser228 'attacks' (nucleophilic) → ester bond 'breaks' — 'classic' triad (serine hydrolase)";
- **"'Role of calcium'"**: Ca²⁺ binds C2 domain → neutralizes membrane surface negative charge + conformational change → enzyme "goes to membrane" — "calcium 'bridge' (C2 domain-membrane) → enzyme 'sticks' to membrane → scissors 'can reach' substrate — 'calcium' is the 'trigger'";

**"'Lysophospholipid' 'signaling'"**: Hydrolysis product lysophospholipid (LPC, etc.) is also a "signaling molecule" (pro-inflammatory/chemotactic) → "dual products, dual signals (AA + lysophospholipid) — 'one cut', two 'messages'";

**"'Downstream fate' of 'AA'"**: Released AA is either "re-esterified" (returns to membrane, cycling) or "metabolized" (LOX/COX/CYP) → balance determines "net release" — "AA 'goes' two ways (back to membrane/metabolism) — 'cycling' and 'consumption' balance — 'net' fuel 'amount' is key";

### 2.1 Structure Overview: The "Relay" of Two Domains

- **"'C2 domain' (1RLW)"**: N-terminal ~140 residues, β-sandwich fold, calcium-binding loops (3 Ca²⁺ sites) → senses calcium + binds membrane phospholipid — "C2 'radar' (senses calcium) + 'anchor' (binds membrane) — 'advance party'";
- **"'Catalytic domain' (1CJY)"**: C-terminal ~550 residues, α/β hydrolase fold (central β-sheet + surrounding α-helices) → active site (Ser228, etc.) → cleaves AA — "catalytic domain 'scissors' (Ser228) — 'main force'";
- **"'Linker' and 'cooperation'"**: C2 domain (membrane) → catalytic domain (active site faces membrane) → "relay" to cleave membrane phospholipid — "C2 'positions' (membrane) → catalytic 'starts cutting' (facing membrane) — 'cooperation' (anchoring + catalysis integrated)";

**"'C2 domain' 'details'"**: 1RLW shows C2 domain is a "β-sandwich" (two β-sheets) + top "calcium-binding loops" (3 Ca²⁺) → calcium bridges to membrane — "β-sandwich 'core' + calcium loop 'grip' (3 Ca²⁺) — 'radar's' 'antenna' (calcium loop)";

**"'Catalytic domain' 'details'"**: 1CJY shows catalytic domain with "central β-sheet + surrounding α-helices" (α/β hydrolase fold) + active site "deep cleft" (Ser228, etc.) → substrate "enters cleft" and is cleaved — "β-sheet 'framework' + helices 'walls' → active 'deep cleft' (Ser228) — 'scissors'' 'edge' (deep cleft)".

### 2.2 Structural Comparison of the Two Domains

| Domain | Position | Fold | Function | Anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C2 domain | N-terminal | β-sandwich | Calcium sensing + membrane binding | 1RLW (2.40 Å) |

**"Structural 'insight'"**: cPLA2α is a "dual-domain 'sensor-effector'" design — "C2 'senses' (calcium/membrane) + catalytic 'executes' (lipid cleavage) — 'signal' (calcium) drives 'action' (AA cleavage) — 'modular' design (domain 'division of labor') — 'regulatable' 'enzyme' (calcium-gated)";

**"'Structure' and 'signaling' 'closed loop'"**: Calcium → C2 to membrane → catalytic cleaves AA → AA metabolism → inflammation → (signaling continues) → "structure 'executes' signal (calcium → action) — 'signal-structure-function' closed loop — 'enzyme' is the 'executor' of 'signal'";

**"'1CJY vs 1RLW' 'complementarity'"**: Catalytic domain (1CJY, 2.50 Å) provides the "scissors" structure; C2 domain (1RLW, 2.40 Å) provides the "radar" structure → together they complete the full picture of the "master valve" (missing the linker region) — "one 'scissors' (1CJY) + one 'radar' (1RLW) → 'master valve' puzzle 'complete' (missing linker, awaiting full-length) — 'split-domain' structures 'complementary'".

## 3. The Molecular Logic of "Calcium → Membrane → Lipid Cleavage": From Structure to Chemistry

### 3.0 Atom by Atom: How Calcium "Opens the Valve"

- **"The 'Pocket' of 'Calcium Binding'"**: The C2 domain calcium-binding loop (acidic residues Asp/Asn) coordinates Ca²⁺ (3 sites) → neutralizes negative charge + stabilizes conformation — "Calcium 'sits' into the 'acid pocket' (Asp coordination) → negative charge 'neutralized' — 'calcium' in place";
- **"The 'Drive' of 'Membrane Binding'"**: Ca²⁺ binding → C2 domain surface "becomes positively charged" + hydrophobic residues exposed → interacts with membrane "negatively charged phospholipids + hydrophobic core" → anchoring — "Calcium 'charges' (C2 positive) → attracts 'membrane' (negatively charged phospholipids) — 'electrostatic + hydrophobic' membrane anchoring";
- **"The 'Orientation' of the 'Catalytic Domain'"**: C2 anchors to membrane → catalytic domain active site "faces" the membrane surface → substrate (membrane phospholipid) enters hydrophobic channel — "Anchor 'orients' (C2 positioning) → scissors 'aimed at' membrane → sn-2 'in position' — 'spatial' coordination";

**"Calcium 'Concentration' Threshold"**: Resting intracellular Ca²⁺ (~100 nM) → cPLA2α "not activated"; stimulation → Ca²⁺ rises (μM) → activation — "Calcium 'water level' reaches threshold (μM) → valve 'opens' — 'threshold' gating (closed at rest / open upon stimulation) — 'safety' switch";

**"The 'Broad Spectrum' of the 'C2 Domain'"**: The C2 domain is a "universal" membrane-binding module (PKC/synaptotagmin etc. all have it) → cPLA2α uses "the same module" — "C2 'universal part' (shared by multiple proteins) — 'modular' evolution — 'assembly' flexibility (swap catalytic domain = new function)".

### 3.1 "Substrate 'Recognition': How It Specifically Cleaves AA"

- **"'sn-2' 'Selectivity'"**: The sn-2 position "prefers" polyunsaturated fatty acids (AA/EPA/DHA) → AA-enriched phospholipids "prioritized" — "sn-2 'picks' (prefers PUFA) → AA phospholipids 'prioritized' for cleavage — 'preference' is 'regulation'";
- **"Channel 'Accommodation'"**: The catalytic domain hydrophobic channel "accommodates" the sn-2 acyl chain (bent PUFA) → conformational "fit" with AA — "Channel 'accommodates' AA chain (bent C20:4) → 'fit' enables 'cleavage' — 'shape' selection";
- **"Lysophospholipid 'Release'"**: After hydrolysis, AA + lysophospholipid are "released" → lysophospholipid is also a "signaling molecule" (pro-inflammatory) → "Kill two birds with one stone" (AA + lysophospholipid, dual signal) — 'products' both 'do work'";

**"Hypothesis on the 'Mechanism' of 'Preference'"**: sn-2 preference for PUFA may be due to "channel shape" (bent chain fit) or "enzyme–substrate" recognition (head group/chain length) → not fully settled — "Why 'pick' AA (channel shape? recognition?) — 'mechanism' not fully 'settled' — 'structure–function' awaits study";

**"Inhibitor 'Binding'"**: cPLA2α inhibitors (arylsulfonamide class) bind the "active site groove" (mimic substrate/transition state) → block hydrolysis — "Inhibitor 'plugs' the scissors 'groove' (mimics substrate) → cannot cleave — 'groove' is 'drug target' (structure-guided design)".

### 3.2 "cPLA2α and LOX/COX 'Upstream–Downstream': One Valve Controls Three Pathways"

- **"'Upstream' (cPLA2α, this article)"**: releases AA — "Master valve 'releases material' (AA)";
- **"'Downstream' (LOX/COX)"**: AA → leukotrienes ([40-lox.md](40-lox.md)) / prostaglandins ([42-cox2.md](42-cox2.md)) — "Branch pathways 'process' (LOX/COX)";
- **"Significance of 'Coupling'"**: cPLA2α releasing AA is "rate-limiting" (downstream substrate-dependent) → inhibiting cPLA2α = "killing multiple birds with one stone" (both leukotrienes + prostaglandins ↓) → "Upstream 'leverage' (one valve controls multiple pathways) — more 'comprehensive' than 'single downstream target' — 'source' intervention 'logic'";

**"'Quantitative' Evidence of 'Coupling'"**: cPLA2α knockout/inhibition → leukotrienes + prostaglandins "both significantly ↓" (cells/animals) → "Empirical evidence of upstream 'leverage' — 'one valve' controls 'two pathways' (both LOX/COX products decrease) — 'source' strategy 'feasible'";

**"'Therapeutic' 'Window'"**: cPLA2α inhibition "partial" (preserve basal AA cycling) → anti-inflammatory "without depletion" — "Close valve 'seven-tenths' (partial inhibition) → fire 'small' but not 'extinguished' (preserve physiology) — 'window' (partial inhibition) is 'key' — 'complete' valve closure 'dangerous' (protective mediators cut off)".
