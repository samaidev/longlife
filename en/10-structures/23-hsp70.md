## 0. Structure at a Glance

**In one sentence**: Hsp70 is the cell's "protein folding rescue team"—an ATP-dependent molecular chaperone that binds hydrophobic segments of nascent polypeptide chains/misfolded proteins, helping them fold correctly and preventing aggregation through a "bind–release" cycle—"the cell's 'folding coach,' teaching proteins how to fold right and pulling back the ones about to fold wrong." It is the core executor of the heat shock response (HSR) and the "quality control–rescue" step in the "protein economy" chain.

```text
Hsp70 (DnaK) Structure:
  NBD (Nucleotide-Binding Domain, ~44 kDa): ATPase activity, "motor"
  SBD (Substrate-Binding Domain, ~25 kDa):  Binds hydrophobic peptides, "grip"
  Linker: The "hinge" for allosteric communication
                  │
  ATP Cycle (Bind-Release Motor):
  ATP binding → SBD open (fast capture, weak binding) → Substrate binding/release
  ATP hydrolysis → ADP binding → SBD closed (firm grip) → Substrate "locked" for folding
  NEF (Nucleotide Exchange Factor, GrpE) → ADP→ATP exchange → SBD reopens → Substrate release
```

**Three Key Structural Features** (the atomic basis determining its function):
1. **Two-domain architecture**: NBD (ATPase) + SBD (substrate binding)—"motor + grip" separated;
2. **Allosteric communication**: ATP hydrolysis → conformational change (NBD→SBD hinge motion) → substrate binding affinity switch—"motor turns, grip opens and closes";
3. **Substrate binding mode**: The SBD's "sandwich" pocket binds a 5-residue hydrophobic core—"the grip only grabs hydrophobic segments."

**Core Regulatory Mechanism**:

```text
Nascent peptides/Stress proteins: Hydrophobic segments exposed (unfolded) → Hsp70 binding (rescue)
        │
ATP Cycle: ATP (fast grab, fast release) → ADP (firm grip) → NEF exchange (release)
        │
Outcome: ①Correct folding (spontaneous folding after release) ②Prevent aggregation ③Hand off to Hsp90/Chaperonin (large folding)
    ④Irreparable → Hand off to proteasome/autophagy (degradation & recycling)
```

## 1. Why Hsp70 Structure Matters

Hsp70 is the "hub" of protein homeostasis:

- **"Folding rescue team"**: ~1/3 of proteins require chaperone assistance to fold—"most proteins learn on their own, the tough ones need a 'coach'";
- **"Aggregation firewall"**: During stress (heat/oxidation) proteins unfold → hydrophobic segments exposed → Hsp70 binds → prevents aggregation—"firewall holds back the 'avalanche'";
- **Anti-aging evidence**: Hsp70 expression declines with age → stress resistance↓ → proteotoxicity; Hsp70 overexpression (model organisms) extends lifespan—"insufficient rescue team, proteome collapses";
- **Dialogue with the ribosome** ([22-ribosome.md](22-ribosome.md)): Nascent polypeptide chains emerge from the ribosomal exit tunnel → Hsp70 binds immediately (co-translational folding)—"the coach waits at the end of the production line";
- **Link with proteasome/autophagy** ([20-proteasome.md](20-proteasome.md)/[18-lc3-autophagy.md](18-lc3-autophagy.md)): Irreparable misfolded proteins → chaperone-guided degradation (CHIP ubiquitin ligase)—"rescue fails, hand off to recycling";
- **Heat shock response (HSR) core**: HSF1 activation → Hsp70 upregulation—"during stress, the rescue team expands" (echoing the HSR paragraph in [20-proteasome.md](20-proteasome.md));
- **Disease connections**: Neurodegeneration (aggregate clearance failure), cancer (chaperones hijacked by cancer cells)—"rescue team dysregulation, a body full of disease."

**Three key questions for structural research**:
1. How does Hsp70 "grab" substrates (atomic mechanism of the SBD)?
2. How does ATP drive the "grab-release" cycle (motor mechanism of allostery)?
3. How does it distinguish "repairable" from "should be degraded" (quality decisions)?

These questions were answered in structures such as 2KHO (full-length DnaK + substrate, 2009), 1DKG (GrpE + NBD, 1997).

## 2. Structural Anatomy of Hsp70

### 2.1 NBD (Nucleotide-Binding Domain) — The "Motor"

- Position: Hsp70 N-terminus (~1-380 residues, ~44 kDa);
- Structure: Two large subdomains (I/II), each containing a "layered β-sandwich + α-helix"—"two lobes clamping ATP";
- ATP binding: Nucleotide-binding pocket between subdomains I/II—"the motor's combustion chamber";
- ATP hydrolysis: ATP→ADP (slow, requires Hsp40/J-protein stimulation)—"the motor's power stroke";
- 1DKG findings: GrpE (NEF) binds NBD → subdomain rotation → nucleotide release—"the fuel-changing mechanism."

### 2.2 SBD (Substrate-Binding Domain) — The "Grip"

- Position: Hsp70 C-terminus (~390-600 residues, ~25 kDa);
- Structure: β-sandwich (substrate-binding pocket) + α-helix lid—"palm + lid";
- Substrate binding: Binds a 5-residue hydrophobic core (e.g., NRLLLTG, 2KHO)—"only grabs hydrophobic segments";
- Lid switch: ATP state lid open (fast grab, fast release); ADP state lid closed (firm grip)—"palm opening and closing controlled by the motor";
- 2KHO findings: Hinge movement between NBD/SBD in full-length DnaK (±35° cone) → allosteric communication—"the motor and grip are 'soft-linked'."

### 2.3 Allosteric Communication — "Motor→Grip Transmission"

- **ATP state**: NBD binds ATP → SBD lid open + low substrate affinity (fast binding, fast release)—"motor idling, grip grabs randomly";
- **ADP state**: ATP hydrolysis → conformational change transmitted via Linker to SBD → lid closed + high substrate affinity (firm grip)—"motor does work, grip locks";
- **NEF exchange**: ADP release → ATP rebinding → SBD reopens → substrate release—"change fuel, grip releases";
- **Hsp40 (J-protein)**: Hands substrate to Hsp70 + stimulates ATP hydrolysis—"the coach's assistant, responsible for passing the ball";
- **"Significance of the cycle"**: The bind-release cycle lets the substrate "repeatedly attempt folding" (each release gives it a folding opportunity) → increases folding success probability—"repeated trial and error until correctly folded."

## 3. Molecular Logic of the Chaperone Cycle: From Structure to Chemistry

### 3.00 The Engineering Wisdom of the "ATP Motor": Why the Hold-Release Cycle Uses ATP

**"Why ATP?"** Substrate binding and release themselves do not consume energy (binding relies on hydrophobic interactions, release on concentration gradients)—but the "directionality of the cycle" requires energy: ATP hydrolysis provides a "ratchet"—**"Without ATP, the cycle would 'run backward' or 'get stuck'; ATP makes the cycle unidirectional and orderly"**—"The motor's role is not 'having strength', but 'having rhythm'."

**"The 'slowness' of ATP hydrolysis is a feature."** Hsp70's ATPase activity is "slow" (~0.05-0.1 min⁻¹, requiring Hsp40 to stimulate and accelerate it ~1000-fold)—"Low base RPM, only fast when stepping on the gas (Hsp40)"—this "slowness" allows the cell to "regulate the cycle frequency" (Hsp40 concentration/activity = gas pedal)—"Only with adjustable RPM can it match the folding needs of different substrates."

**"The 'spring' of allostery."** ATP hydrolysis in the NBD → closure of the two large subdomains (I/II) → transmitted via the Linker to the SBD → lid closure—"the combustion chamber does work, transmitted via the connecting rod to the gripper"—2KHO shows the Linker is a "flexible hinge" (±35° conical motion)—"the connecting rod is soft, allowing a wide range of adjustment"—"soft connecting rod = adjustable allosteric amplitude."

**"GrpE's 'lockpicking'."** NEF (GrpE, 1DKG) binds to the NBD → subdomain rotation (outward ~14°) → nucleotide release (ADP falls out) → ATP rebinds—"a mechanic uses a crowbar to pry out the old fuel"—the long α-helix of the GrpE dimer also "reaches" the SBD → participates in substrate release—"picking the lock + pushing the door, a two-pronged approach."

### 3.0 Looking Atom by Atom: Why Hsp70 Needs to "Hold-Release-Hold"

**"Folding is a 'trial and error' process."** Spontaneous protein folding requires "exploring conformational space" (searching for the lowest energy state)—if the chaperone holds on constantly, the substrate cannot explore → it will never fold—"if the coach always holds the student, the student never learns to walk."

**"The hold-release cycle = iterative refolding attempts."** Each release → the substrate has a "free exploration window" (attempts to fold); if folding succeeds → hydrophobic segments are buried internally → no longer recognized by Hsp70 (signal disappears) → cycle terminates; if folding fails → hydrophobic segments are exposed again → caught by Hsp70 again → released again—"repeated trial and error until it folds correctly or gives up."

**"ATP dictates the rhythm."** The cycle frequency is determined by the ATP hydrolysis/exchange rate (seconds scale)—"the coach's rhythm of 'letting go and holding again' is controlled by the motor"—too fast (releasing too frequently) → not enough folding opportunities; too slow (holding without letting go) → exploration is limited—"the rhythm must be just right."

**"The wisdom of hydrophobic recognition."** Hsp70 only catches "exposed hydrophobic segments"—correctly folded proteins have their hydrophobic segments internally (not exposed) → not caught—"the coach only catches students who 'expose their flaws', not those who are well-folded"—this is a "quality signal" (hydrophobic exposure = unfolded)—"degree of hydrophobic exposure = a natural indicator of folding quality."

### 3.1 The "Chaperone Network": Hsp70 Does Not Fight Alone

- **Hsp40 (J-protein)**: Hands the substrate to Hsp70 + stimulates ATP hydrolysis—"assistant passes the ball + steps on the gas";
- **GrpE/Bag-1 (NEF)**: Promotes ADP→ATP exchange—"mechanic changing the fuel";
- **Hsp90**: Receives "half-folded" substrates from Hsp70 → completes late-stage folding (especially signaling proteins/kinases)—"advanced coach takes over";
- **Chaperonin (Hsp60/GroEL)**: Cage-like structure, provides "isolated folding chamber" (folding space for large proteins)—"closed training camp";
- **CHIP (E3 ligase)**: Irreparable substrates → ubiquitination → proteasome—"rescue fails, hand off to recycling";
- **p62/autophagy adaptor**: Aggregates → autophagy ([18-lc3-autophagy.md](18-lc3-autophagy.md))—"large trash to the garbage station."

**"The network's decision logic"**: Hsp70 binds substrate → attempts folding (cycle) → success (release) or failure (after multiple cycles) → hand off to Hsp90 (retry) or CHIP (degradation)—"tiered processing: first rescue, then escalate, finally give up"—**"the chaperone network is the protein's 'quality court': repair what can be repaired, sentence the irreparable to 'death' (degradation)"**.

### 3.20 "Hsp90's Relay": The "Late-Stage Processing Factory" of the Chaperone Network

**"Hsp70→Hsp90 handoff."** Hsp70 first hands "half-folded" substrates to Hsp90 (via Hop/STI1 adaptor protein)—"the初级 coach hands the student to the advanced coach."

**"Hsp90's clients."** Hsp90 mainly serves "signaling proteins/kinases/steroid receptors" (e.g., hormone receptors, SRC kinase, telomerase)—"advanced students: proteins requiring fine assembly"—Hsp90 substrates are often "regulatory nodes" (once dysregulated → cancer)—"the advanced coach teaches the 'management'."

**"Hsp90's ATP cycle."** Hsp90 is also an ATPase (dimer, clamp-like conformational change: open→closed→release)—"the advanced coach also uses an ATP cycle"—Hsp90 inhibitors (geldanamycin class) are anti-cancer drugs—"attacking the cancer cell's 'advanced coach'."

**"Hsp90 and aging."** Hsp90 levels/function change with age → signaling protein maturation affected (hormone/stress response); Hsp90 is also "an accomplice of cancer cells" (stabilizing mutant oncogenic proteins)—"the advanced coach is retained by cancer cells."

**"The synergistic significance of the network"**: Hsp70 (general rescue) + Hsp90 (fine processing) + chaperonin (isolated folding) → covers different folding needs—"the rescue team has 'generalists' and 'specialists'—Hsp70 is the emergency generalist, Hsp90 is the specialist clinic, chaperonin is the isolation ward"—**"protein folding is not 'one coach', it's 'a complete training system'"**.

### 3.3 The Molecular Logic of "Quality Decisions": Rescue or Kill

**"CHIP's competition."** CHIP (E3 ligase) simultaneously binds Hsp70/Hsp90 and the ubiquitination machinery → ubiquitinates "rescue-failed" substrates → proteasome—"the rescue team's 'military doctor' rules: can't be saved, sign the death certificate (ubiquitin tag)."

**"Cycle count = verdict basis."** Substrate repeatedly attempts in the Hsp70 cycle → if it consistently cannot fold correctly (hydrophobic segments persistently exposed) → CHIP competitively "grabs" the substrate → ubiquitination—"after several rounds of failure, sentence to 'death'."

**"Regulation of quality decisions."** During stress (Hsp70↑) → rescue tendency enhanced (CHIP competition weakened)—"more hands, rescue more times"; during aging (Hsp70↓) → rescue tendency weakened → substrates prematurely sent to degradation or aggregation—"fewer hands, false death sentences or missed judgments."

**"Anti-aging insight"**: Maintain the chaperone-degradation balance (sufficient rescue + agile recycling) → quality decisions are "just"—"neither over-rescuing bad proteins (aggregation) nor under-rescuing good proteins (waste)"—"the quality court must be 'just', relying on the health of the entire pipeline."

## 4. Hsp70 and Aging: A Structural Perspective on "Rescue Team Aging"

### 4.1 Age-Related Decline of Hsp70

- **Decreased expression**: Decreased Hsp70 induction capacity in aged tissues (↓ HSF1 activity) → insufficient expansion of the rescue team during stress—"the alarm sounds, but the rescue team doesn't come";
- **Decreased activity**: Oxidation/modification of Hsp70 itself → ↓ ATPase activity → slowed cycling—"the coach is old and moves slowly";
- **Chaperone network dysregulation**: Altered levels of Hsp40/Hsp90/CHIP → network imbalance—"the entire rescue system is aging";
- **Consequences**: Accumulation of misfolded proteins → aggregation → proteotoxicity (neurodegeneration/sarcopenia)—"poor rescue leads to mountains of defective goods."

### 4.2 Anti-Aging Strategies: "Repairing the Rescue Team"

- **Heat shock response induction (hormesis)**: Mild stress (exercise/heat exposure/fasting) → induces Hsp70—"a small alarm expands the rescue team" (echoing the HSR paragraph in [20-proteasome.md](20-proteasome.md));
- **Pharmacological chaperone induction**: Hydroxylamine derivatives/sodium butyrate, etc. → induces Hsp70—"drugs expand the rescue team";
- **Reducing stress burden**: Glucose control/antioxidants → fewer misfolded proteins generated → rescue team not overloaded—"fewer accidents mean a less tired rescue team";
- **Enhancing downstream degradation** ([20-proteasome.md](20-proteasome.md)/[18-lc3-autophagy.md](18-lc3-autophagy.md)): Rescue failure → efficient recycling—"if it can't be saved, clear it away promptly";
- **The principle of "moderation"**: Hsp70 overexpression (in cancer cells) → protects cancer cells (anti-apoptotic)—"the rescue team is also exploited by cancer cells"—"expand moderately, don't let cancer cells benefit too."

**"The complete logic of the chain"**: Ribosome production (synthesis) → Hsp70 quality control/rescue (folding) → proteasome/autophagy recycling (degradation)—**a closed loop of "production-quality control-recycling"**—all three steps decline during aging → proteome collapse—"when the entire industrial chain is paralyzed, the cell becomes old."

### 6.00 "Pipeline Rhythm": "Shift Management" of the Protein Economy

**"Circadian rhythm."** Protein synthesis/degradation has circadian fluctuations (mTOR signaling, autophagy rhythm)—"the factory has day and night shifts"—nighttime autophagy↑ (repair) → "the night shift is the maintenance shift"—late nights/shift work disrupts rhythm → proteostasis impaired—"shifts disrupted, factory has accidents."

**"Feeding rhythm."** Post-meal synthesis↑ (mTOR); between meals/fasting periods autophagy↑ (AMPK/FOXO3)—"produce at mealtime, maintain during fasting"—continuous eating → no maintenance window → defective product accumulation—"always producing without maintenance, machines break sooner or later."

**"Exercise rhythm."** Exercise induces stress → chaperones↑ (HSR) + autophagy↑ (AMPK)—"regular 'drills + major cleaning'"—post-exercise recovery synthesis↑ (muscle repair)—"train, then repair, then replenish."

**"Intermittent vs. continuous."** Intermittent fasting/exercise (rhythmic stress) → "pulsed optimization" of protein economy (alternating production-maintenance); continuous overeating/sedentary → "non-stop production, insufficient maintenance"—"the factory needs alternating 'production-maintenance', not constant production."

**"Anti-aging rhythm prescription"**: ① Intermittent fasting ([03-nutrition](../07-interventions/03-nutrition.md)) → give maintenance windows; ② Regular exercise → scheduled drills + major cleaning; ③ Regular sleep → normal night-shift maintenance—"protein economy health = rhythm health"—**"not 'more production' or 'more recycling', but 'alternating by rhythm'—this is the core principle repeatedly appearing in the 10-structures series, and the essence of protein economy 'shift management'"**.

## 7. Structure-Aging "Five Questions" Summary

| Question | Structural Answer |
|---|---|
| How does Hsp70 capture substrates? | The β-sandwich pocket of the SBD binds the 5-peptide hydrophobic core + lid locking—"palm + lid" |
| How does ATP drive the cycle? | ATP state opens lid for fast capture and release → hydrolysis leads to ADP state closing lid for firm grip → NEF exchange reopens |
| Why "catch-release-catch"? | Repeated release gives folding chances (trial and error)—"you must let go to learn to walk" |
| Why does Hsp70 decline with age? | Decreased induction capacity (HSF1) + self-damage—"insufficient rescue team expansion + veterans retiring" |
| How to counter aging? | Mild stress (exercise/heat) induction + reducing burden + enhancing downstream recycling—"expand + reduce burden + safety net" |

**Structural intuition beyond the five questions**: The story of Hsp70 reveals the "redundancy philosophy of biological systems"—**"for the most critical functions, evolution always keeps 'multiple plans'"**. Protein folding is so crucial that cells prepare a three-tiered defense: Hsp70 (general rescue) → Hsp90/Chaperonin (specialized handling) → degradation and recycling (safety net)—"if one layer fails, there is another." This "redundancy" is not waste, but **"fault tolerance"**—single-point failures won't cause a crash (if one chaperone breaks, others step in)—"backups are the price of reliability, and the guarantee of longevity." **Anti-aging implications**: ① Maintain "redundancy" (don't let any layer completely fail) → resilient proteome—"if all three defense lines are intact, aging slows down"; ② Mild stress (hormesis) activates "redundancy response" (HSF1 upregulates the full chaperone suite) → enhanced resilience—"regular drills keep backups on standby"—**"one essence of anti-aging is maintaining cellular 'redundancy and fault tolerance'—important machinery must have backups"**.

### 8.1 The Complete Picture of "Chaperones-Aging": From HSR to Neurodegeneration

**"Aging of HSF1."** HSF1 (master switch for chaperone genes) activity declines with age (nuclear localization↓ / DNA binding↓) → decreased stress induction capacity—"the commander gets old, expansion orders can't be issued."

**"Chaperone Depletion Hypothesis."** Chronic proteotoxicity (accumulation of aggregation-prone proteins) → chaperones are "siphoned off" (binding aggregation intermediates) → insufficient free chaperones → other proteins lose protection → more misfolding → vicious cycle—"the rescue team is tied up by a 'major incident', leaving no one to handle other incidents."

**"Chaperone Perspective on Neurodegeneration."** AD (Aβ/Tau aggregation), PD (α-synuclein) → chaperone depletion + insufficient HSR → uncontrolled aggregation—"the rescue team is overwhelmed, neurons die first."

**"Chaperone Perspective on Longevity."** Long-lived animals/model organisms often have stronger HSR/chaperone levels (e.g., high-molecular-weight Hsp70 in naked mole-rats)—"the 'rescue team' of long-lived species is stronger."

**"Window of Intervention"**: ① **Preventive hormesis** (starting exercise/heat exposure in middle age) → maintains HSR sensitivity—"train the rescue team well while young"; ② **Early intervention** (inducing chaperones in early neurodegeneration) → prevents aggregation—"reinforce before the avalanche"; ③ **Combination strategies** (chaperone induction + autophagy enhancement + anti-inflammation) → multiple defense lines—"rescue + recycling + firefighting, advancing on three fronts"—"chaperone-based anti-aging is not 'one drug', but 'maintaining the entire rescue system'."
