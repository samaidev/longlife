# 01 NO cardiovascular protection mechanism

> Core point of view: Endothelium-derived nitric oxide (NO) is the "universal protective signal" of the cardiovascular system - vasodilation, anti-atherosclerosis, anti-thrombosis, and endothelial protection. The essence of vascular aging is, to a large extent, the progressive decline of "NO bioavailability". This chapter reviews the entire chain of the eNOS/NO/cGMP pathway, pathological damage mechanisms, and interventional targets.

## 0. NO Discovery History and Dual Pathway Framework

In 1980, Furchgott discovered that the vasodilation of acetylcholine depends on the intact endothelium ("endothelium-dependent relaxation"). In 1987, two teams, Palmer and Ignarro, respectively identified the endothelium-derived relaxing factor (EDRF), which is NO - a discovery that won the 1998 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. There are **two pathways** for NO production in the human body: ① the classic enzymatic pathway (L-arginine/eNOS, consuming O₂ and BH4, see Section 1 for details); ② the dietary nitrate non-enzymatic bypass (nitrate → nitrite → NO, active in hypoxic and acidic environments, see article 02 for details). The two pathways are complementary: exercise and shear stress activate the former; diet and hypoxia activate the latter. Understanding "NO bioavailability" requires mastering both the synthesis and clearance kinetics of both.

## 1. eNOS/NO/cGMP signaling pathway: from shear stress to vasodilation

### 1.1 Full chain molecular events

Vascular endothelial cells continue to feel the **shear stress** generated by blood flow friction, which is the first level switch for eNOS activation under physiological conditions. Laminar shear stress (approximately 10–20 dyn/cm²) activates eNOS through three parallel pathways:

```text
血流剪切应力 (10-20 dyn/cm²)
   │
   ├─→ 机械感受复合体: PECAM-1(胞外感应) → VEGFR2(信号放大) → VE-钙黏蛋白(支架)
   │        │ 整合素 αvβ3 / α5β1 锚定细胞外基质, 传递张力
   │        ▼
   │     Src → PI3K (p85/p110) → PIP2 → PIP3
   │        ▼
   │     PDK1 → Akt (Thr308 磷酸化) ─┐
   │        │                        │ 第二信号
   │        ▼                        ▼
   │     eNOS 活性位点: Ser1177(激活↑) / Thr495(抑制↓)
   │        │ 热休克蛋白 Hsp90 作分子伴侣, 钙调蛋白 CaM 结合
   │        ▼
   │     L-精氨酸 + O2 ──eNOS──→ L-瓜氨酸 + NO
   │
   └─→ [旁路] 剪切应力 → G蛋白偶联受体 → PLC → IP3 → Ca²⁺↑ → CaM → eNOS
   └─→ [旁路] AMPK / PKA 也可直接磷酸化 eNOS-Ser1177
```

The generated NO diffuses into smooth muscle cells (half-life only 1–5 seconds, diffusion distance approximately 100–200 µm), activating soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC):

```text
NO + sGC 血红素铁 → sGC 构象激活(约 200-400 倍) → GTP → cGMP
   ▼
cGMP 激活 PKG-I (蛋白激酶 G)
   ├─→ 磷酸化肌球蛋白轻链激酶(MLCK) 使其失活 → MLC 去磷酸化 → 平滑肌松弛
   ├─→ 激活肌浆网 SERCA → 胞浆 Ca²⁺↓ → 松弛
   ├─→ 开放 BKCa 钾通道 → 膜超极化 → L型钙通道关闭 → Ca²⁺内流↓
   └─→ 磷酸化 RhoA → Rho 激酶信号抑制 → 收缩敏化下降
```

**Quantification anchor**: Under physiological conditions, the endothelium can release approximately 1 µmol NO per minute; cGMP production efficiency increases approximately 200–400 times after sGC is activated by NO; nitrovasodilators such as nitroglycerin work through this pathway, and their effects can be completely blocked by sGC inhibitors (such as ODQ).

### 1.2 Final execution of vasodilation: Myosin light chain dephosphorylation

Smooth muscle contraction is driven by phosphorylation of myosin light chain (MLC). The NO pathway jointly reduces MLC phosphorylation levels through three parallel mechanisms: ① PKG phosphorylates the inhibitory site of MLCK, directly reducing its activity; ② reducing the cytoplasmic Ca²⁺ concentration (SERCA uptake↑ + potassium channel hyperpolarization), indirectly weakening Ca²⁺-CaM-MLCK activation; ③ inhibiting the RhoA/ROCK pathway, releasing its inhibition of myosin phosphatase, allowing the phosphatase to be dephosphorylated MLC. Three-pronged approach, blood vessel dilation.

### 1.3 Grading of evidence

| Evidence | Content | Level |
|---|---|---|
| eNOS knockout mice | blood pressure increased by 15–20 mmHg and the diastolic response to acetylcholine was lost | A (genetic model) |
| eNOS-Ser1177 phosphorylation site mutant mice | Shear stress-induced diastolic function is significantly impaired | B (mechanism study) |
| sGC agonist (riociguat) | clinically approved for pulmonary arterial hypertension, validating cGMP pathway pharmacological target | A (RCT) |
| PDE5 inhibitor (sildenafil) | Enhances NO signaling by inhibiting cGMP hydrolysis, ED/pulmonary hypertension indication | A (RCT) |

## 2. Anti-atherosclerotic effect

Atherosclerosis begins with endothelial dysfunction. NO exerts an inhibitory function at every key node:

```text
LDL ──氧化──→ oxLDL ──结合 LOX-1(内皮/巨噬细胞)──→ 摄取↑
   ▲                                          │
   │ NO 抑制 LDL 氧化                          ▼
   │                                       泡沫细胞 ← 单核细胞→巨噬细胞
   │                                          ▲
   │                              NO 抑制 VCAM-1/ICAM-1 表达 → 单核细胞黏附↓
   ▼
内皮损伤 → 平滑肌细胞增殖/迁移 (PDGF 驱动) ←── NO 抑制 (cGMP-PKG 通路)
   │
   └─→ 内皮间质转化 EndMT (TGF-β/Snail) ←── NO/NOX4 信号维持内皮表型, 抑制 EndMT
```

- **Anti-LDL oxidation**: NO directly scavenges lipid peroxidation free radical chains and inhibits the oxidation of LDL to oxLDL; when endothelial-derived NO is sufficient, the production of oxLDL is significantly reduced.
- **Blocking oxLDL/LOX-1 signaling**: oxLDL binding to the scavenger receptor LOX-1 will promote endothelial apoptosis, inflammation and foam cell formation; upregulation of NO can inhibit LOX-1 expression and its downstream NF-κB inflammatory pathway.
- **Inhibits leukocyte adhesion**: NO inhibits NF-κB nuclear translocation, downregulates the expression of adhesion molecules VCAM-1, ICAM-1 and E-selectin, and blocks monocyte rolling-adhesion-transendothelial migration. The area of ​​atherosclerotic lesions in eNOS knockout mice is significantly increased.
- **Inhibits Smooth Muscle Proliferation**: The NO/cGMP/PKG pathway inhibits PDGF-induced smooth muscle cell proliferation and migration (interference in cell cycle G1/S transition) and maintains vascular media homeostasis.
- **Inhibition of endothelial-mesenchymal transition (EndMT)**: Under TGF-β or inflammatory stimulation, endothelial cells can transdifferentiate into mesenchymal cells (expressing α-SMA, type I collagen), participating in fibrosis and plaque progression; NO signaling is an important negative regulator of endothelial phenotype maintenance.

**Evidence Grading**: Statins improve endothelial function (increased FMD) and are associated with NO bioavailability - Grade A (RCT meta); eNOS deficiency accelerates atherosclerosis in ApoE-/- mice - Grade A (genetic model); clinical trials of L-arginine supplementation have mixed results (some have shown improvement in FMD, some have been ineffective or even harmful) - Grade B/C.

## 3. Anti-thrombotic effect

| Links | Mechanism | Results |
|---|---|---|
| Platelet activation | NO activates sGC/cGMP/PKG in platelets, inhibiting P-selectin exposure and αIIbβ3 (GPIIb/IIIa) conformational activation | Platelet adhesion and aggregation↓ |
| Platelet-endothelial adhesion | NO inhibits vWF-mediated binding of platelets to endothelium/collagen | Initial thrombosis↓ |
| Tissue factor | NO downregulates TF expression in endothelial and monocyte cells (inhibiting NF-κB) | Initiates exogenous coagulation↓ |
| Fibrin formation | Reduced thrombin generation → Fibrin deposition↓ | Thrombus stability↓ |

The blood flow shear stress gradient explains a classic phenomenon: **Atherosclerotic plaques tend to occur at blood vessel bifurcations with low shear stress**—where eNOS activation is insufficient, NO production is low, and platelets and white blood cells are more likely to adhere. The antithrombotic effect of NO is an extension of "blood flow protection of the endothelium".

## 4. Endothelial barrier function and endothelial progenitor cells

- **Barrier function**: The NO/cGMP/PKG pathway stabilizes the assembly of VE-cadherin and connexins (claudin-5, occludin), inhibiting the disassembly of adherens junctions induced by inflammatory factors; when NO is reduced, endothelial permeability increases, making it easier for LDL and inflammatory cells to enter the intima. Pulmonary vascular permeability is significantly increased in eNOS knockout mice.
- **Endothelial Progenitor Cells (EPC)**: NO promotes the mobilization, homing and differentiation of bone marrow EPCs and participates in repair after endothelial injury. Statins and exercise improve the number and function of EPCs, partially mediated through the upregulation of eNOS; angiogenesis after ischemia is significantly impaired in eNOS-deficient mice.
- **Angiogenesis Balance**: Physiological angiogenesis depends on NO (VEGF→eNOS is a key downstream of angiogenesis), but pathological neovascularization (new blood vessels in tumors and plaques) requires careful differentiation of the dual nature of NO.
- **Flow-Mediated Dilation (FMD) as a Functional Readout**: FMD uses ultrasound to measure the percent dilation of the brachial artery caused by post-ischemic reactive hyperemia and is the gold standard non-invasive measure for clinical assessment of "endothelial NO function". The main contribution to its expansion is NO (FMD significantly decreases after L-NAME blockade), so FMD is often used as a surrogate endpoint for nitrate supplementation and exercise intervention (see 02 dose-effect RCT).

## 4.1 Anti-inflammation and immune regulation

The anti-atherosclerotic effect of NO also extends to the inflammatory network: ① inhibits the NF-κB pathway and downregulates the transcription of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6, MCP-1); ② inhibits the expression of endothelial cell adhesion molecules (see Section 2); ③ regulates T cell differentiation toward the Th2/regulatory T cell phenotype and inhibits Th1 pro-inflammatory responses; ④ reduces the polarization of macrophages toward the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype. Together, these effects constitute the homeostasis of "NO sufficiency = silent vascular inflammation." On the contrary, when the bioavailability of NO decreases, the blood vessel wall enters a state of chronic low-grade inflammation, providing soil for atherosclerosis and vascular calcification (see article 05).

## 4.2 NO and plaque stability: from "lumen stenosis" to "plaque vulnerability"

The traditional view is that the danger of atherosclerosis lies in luminal narrowing, but modern cardiology recognizes that: **The main causes of acute myocardial infarction and stroke are plaque rupture and erosion, rather than progressive stenosis**—about 70% of acute coronary syndromes originate from the rupture of "vulnerable plaques" (thin fibrous cap, large lipid core, large infiltration of inflammatory cells). NO plays a key role in maintaining plaque stability:

- **Inhibits fibrous cap degradation**: The plaque fibrous cap is composed of collagen, and its integrity depends on the balance of synthesis (smooth muscle cells) and degradation (matrix metalloproteinases MMP). NO inhibits the expression and activity of MMP-2/MMP-9, while promoting collagen synthesis in smooth muscle cells and protecting the fibrous cap from being "digested".
- **Inhibit macrophage foaming and apoptosis**: NO reduces oxLDL uptake (downregulates scavenger receptor expression) and reduces the proportion of macrophages converted into foam cells; it also inhibits macrophage apoptosis and necrosis (necrotic core is a characteristic of vulnerable plaques), thereby shrinking the lipid core.
- **The "last line of defense" against thrombosis**: After plaque rupture, the exposed tissue factor and collagen immediately trigger platelet aggregation and coagulation. The antiplatelet effect of NO (see Section 3) now becomes key to limiting the size of the thrombus and preventing complete occlusion of the vessel - "Patients with sufficient NO are more likely to develop non-occlusive thrombus even if the plaque ruptures".
- **Vasa vasorum and intra-plaque hemorrhage**: NO inhibits pathological neovascularization (see Section 4), reduces microvessel formation in fragile plaques, and reduces the risk of intra-plaque hemorrhage and rapid progression.

**Clinical significance**: This explains why patients with "stable plaques" (thick fibrous cap, small lipid core) have impaired FMD but lower event rates. It also explains why improving NO function (statins, exercise, smoking cessation) can significantly reduce event rates - their effect is not limited to "shrinking plaques", but also "stabilizing plaques". Intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) and optical coherence tomography (OCT) studies have shown that fibrous cap thickening and lipid core shrinkage can be observed after 6–12 months of statin treatment, paralleling improvements in NO bioavailability.

## 5. eNOS uncoupling: a core failure mode of the NO pathway

When tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4, an essential cofactor of eNOS) is insufficient, the electron transport chain of eNOS "leaks": NO is no longer produced and O2 single electrons are reduced to **superoxide anion (O2⁻)**. This is eNOS uncoupling—a vicious cycle in which "antioxidant enzymes become prooxidant enzymes":

```text
氧化应激 (oxLDL/AGEs/血管紧张素II/高糖)
   │
   ▼
BH4 氧化 → BH2 (7,8-二氢生物蝶呤) 积累
   │  (BH4/BH2 比值下降; BH2 与 BH4 竞争 eNOS 结合位点)
   ▼
eNOS 解偶联 → 产生 O2⁻ 而非 NO
   │        ├─→ O2⁻ + NO → ONOO⁻ (过氧亚硝酸盐, 强氧化剂)
   │        └─→ ONOO⁻ 进一步氧化 BH4 → 解偶联加重 (正反馈)
   ▼
NO 生物利用度崩溃 + 氧化损伤 ↑ (内皮功能障碍, 动脉粥样硬化加速)
```

**Concomitant competition**: Arginase (highly expressed in vascular endothelium) competes with eNOS for the common substrate L-arginine; increased arginase activity (during inflammation/aging) leads to insufficient eNOS substrate, exacerbating uncoupling. In addition, methylated arginine ADMA is an endogenous competitive inhibitor of eNOS, with a normal plasma concentration of approximately 0.4–0.6 µmol/L and a 2–3-fold increase in nephropathy, diabetes, and hyperhomocysteinemia.

**Intervention implications**: BH4 supplementation (poor oral bioavailability), folic acid (stabilizes BH4), statins (upregulates GTPCH-I, the rate-limiting enzyme in BH4 synthesis), and inhibition of arginase have all been explored as strategies to restore eNOS coupling.

## 5.1 NO and hypertension: from mechanism to clinical evidence

Hypertension is one of the most direct clinical consequences of reduced NO bioavailability. The blood pressure of eNOS knockout mice is 15–20 mmHg higher than that of wild type. This genetic model established the causal chain of "NO deficiency → increased peripheral resistance → increased blood pressure". The evidence at the clinical level is equally clear: endothelial dysfunction (decreased FMD) and hypertension reinforce each other, creating a vicious cycle in which hypertension itself further disrupts the NO pathway by increasing oxidative stress.

**Three major mechanisms of NO blood pressure reduction**: ① dilate resistance arterioles and reduce total peripheral resistance (NO is the most important endogenous vasodilator factor in the body); ② inhibit the excessive activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) and reduce angiotensin II-mediated vasoconstriction and aldosterone secretion; ③ promote renal sodium excretion (NO is involved in pressure diuresis and sodium balance regulation, and salt-sensitive hypertension is more prominent in eNOS deficiency).

**Clinical Evidence Chain**:
- Nitrate supplementation: A single intake of 500 mL of beetroot juice (containing approximately 6.4 mmol of nitrate) in healthy individuals reduced systolic blood pressure by approximately 5–10 mmHg after 2.5–3 hours (Webb 2008, Hypertension); patients with hypertension who ingested nitrates for 4 consecutive weeks experienced a reduction in ambulatory blood pressure of 7.7/5.2 mmHg (Kapil 2015, Hypertension).
- Blood pressure dependence of endothelial function: each 1 percentage point decrease in FMD is associated with an approximately 8–13% increase in the risk of cardiovascular events (multiple prospective cohort meta-synthesis); and interventions that improve FMD (exercise, statins, nitrates) are almost always associated with blood pressure benefits.
- The relationship between salt restriction and NO: A high-salt diet not only increases blood pressure, but also directly inhibits eNOS activity and reduces skin NO stores (cutaneous nitrate-nitrite pools). "Salt sensitivity" is partially mediated through the NO pathway.

**Intervention Enlightenment**: Antihypertensive drugs (ACEI/ARB, CCB) and lifestyle intervention (exercise, salt reduction, dietary nitrate) have the same goal in "restoring NO bioavailability" - this explains why the effect of exercise on hypertension is equivalent to that of a single drug (about 5–10 mmHg reduction), and also explains why "exercise + salt restriction + vegetable nitrate" is recommended by the guide as a first-line lifestyle prescription.

## 5.2 NO and metabolic syndrome and diabetes

The association between metabolic syndrome (abdominal obesity, hyperglycemia, dyslipidemia, and hypertension) and the NO pathway is bidirectional:

- **Insulin is the activation signal of eNOS**: After insulin binds to the receptor, it phosphorylates eNOS-Ser1177 via the PI3K/Akt pathway, promotes NO production, and at the same time mediates the increase in skeletal muscle blood flow and glucose uptake - "vasodilation" and "metabolism" are coupled by the same signal. In insulin resistance, this PI3K/Akt/eNOS branch is impaired ("selective insulin resistance"), while the mitogenic MAPK branch is preserved or even enhanced, resulting in decreased NO production and increased endothelin-1 (ET-1, a vasoconstrictor) production—a vasomotor imbalance.
- **NO destruction by hyperglycemia**: High glucose environment damages the NO pathway through multiple pathways - activating the polyol pathway to consume NADPH (restricted regeneration of eNOS cofactor), increasing AGEs production, promoting protein kinase C (PKC) activation and inhibiting eNOS activity, exacerbating oxidative stress leading to BH4 oxidation. The FMD of patients with diabetes is significantly lower than that of healthy people of the same age (approximately 3–5 percentage points lower), and this decrease occurs before clinical complications and is a sensitive indicator of the "prediabetic vascular state".
- **Obesity and ADMA**: Inflammatory factors (TNF-α, IL-6) released from visceral adipose tissue upregulate arginase and inhibit DDAH (ADMA degrading enzyme), plasma ADMA increases 1.5–2 times, directly inhibiting eNOS. Weight loss (especially visceral fat loss) can restore DDAH activity, reduce ADMA, and improve FMD.

**Clinical evidence**: Metformin improves vascular endothelial function (partly through the AMPK→eNOS pathway, see article 07 for details); GLP-1 receptor agonists improve FMD while losing weight (see article 08 for details); "vascular protection" in patients with diabetes is essentially "NO protection".

## 6. Vascular aging: why NO decreases with age

The core phenotype of vascular aging is **endothelial dysfunction + arterial stiffness**, and the mechanism is the decrease in NO bioavailability:

| Mechanism | Pathway | Changes with age |
|---|---|---|
| Oxidative stress | Vascular NADPH oxidase (Nox1/Nox2) activity ↑, O2⁻ directly quenches NO (reaction rate ~10¹⁰ M⁻¹s⁻¹, can occur with limited diffusion) | ↑↑ |
| eNOS uncoupling | BH4 oxidation, arginase↑, substrate competition | ↑ |
| ADMA accumulation | DDAH (ADMA degrading enzyme) activity ↓, ADMA plasma concentration increases with age | ↑ |
| AGEs deposition | Advanced glycation end products cross-link collagen/elastin, and AGE-RAGE signaling activates NF-κB and inhibits eNOS | ↑↑ |
| Endothelial cell senescence | Telomere shortening, cell senescence (senescence) → replicative eNOS expression ↓, inflammatory factor secretion ↑ (SASP) | ↑ |
| Blunted shear stress response | PECAM-1/integrin mechanosensory complex signal attenuation | ↑ |

**Quantitative Anchor**: Brachial artery flow-mediated dilation (FMD) decreases with age by approximately 1–2 percentage points per decade (approximately 8–10% in healthy adults at 30 years of age and 4–5% at 70 years of age); NO synthesis substrates and cofactor stores decline in parallel with the decline in FMD. Tissue accumulation of AGEs can increase several times between the ages of 20 and 80, directly cross-linking the elastin of the arterial wall, and is the structural basis of "stiffness".

## 7. Intervention strategies

| Intervention | Target | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Aerobic exercise | Shear stress ↑ → eNOS-Ser1177 phosphorylation ↑; long-term exercise upregulates eNOS expression and improves FMD | Level A (RCT pool, FMD improvement approximately 3–4%) |
| Dietary nitrate (beetroot, etc.) | Nitrate→nitrite→NO (see article 02 for details); acute supplementation reduces blood pressure by 4–10 mmHg | Grade A/B |
| Statins | Upregulate eNOS, inhibit NADPH oxidase, increase BH4; improve FMD | Grade A |
| ACEI/ARB | Inhibits angiotensin II (which activates Nox via AT1 receptors and inhibits eNOS) | Class A |
| PDE5 inhibitors | Inhibit cGMP hydrolysis and amplify residual NO signaling | Class B (limited evidence of cardiovascular protection) |
| Traditional Chinese medicine | Salvia miltiorrhiza (salvianolic acid), Ligusticum chuanxiong (ligustrazine), Astragalus membranaceus (astragaloside IV), etc. have been reported to upregulate eNOS/antioxidants, but there is insufficient high-quality clinical evidence | Level C |
| Antioxidant supplementation (vitamin C/E) | High-dose acute administration of vitamin C improves FMD (O2⁻ scavenging), but long-term RCTs show no cardiovascular benefit | Grade B/C (routine supplementation not supported) |

**Key conclusion**: The fundamental means to restore the NO pathway is not to "supplement NO itself", but to repair its production environment-reduce oxidative stress and uncoupling, maintain substrates and cofactors, and maintain mechanosensory signals. Exercise and dietary nitrates are the two most cost-effective.

## 7.1 Clinical assessment of vascular aging: FMD and pulse wave velocity

The core of clinical assessment of vascular aging is the two dimensions of "structure" and "function":

- **Functional dimension—flow-mediated dilation (FMD)**: Ultrasound measurement of percent expansion of brachial artery diameter after reactive hyperemia, reflecting endothelium-dependent NO release capacity. In healthy adults, FMD is approximately 8–10% at age 30 years, decreasing by 1–2 percentage points per decade, to approximately 4–5% at age 70 years. The clinical value of FMD: ① independently predicts cardiovascular events (meta-analysis shows that for every 1% decrease in FMD, the risk of events increases by approximately 8–13%); ② serves as a sensitive surrogate endpoint for intervention effects (exercise, statins, and nitrates can all increase by 2–5 percentage points); ③ requires high standardization of operations (strict temperature control, fasting, and resting are required), and clinical promotion is limited by measurement variation.
- **Structural Dimension—Pulse Wave Velocity (PWV) and Central Arterial Pressure**: Carotid-femoral PWV (cfPWV) reflects aortic stiffness, which is normal at about 7–8 m/s at age 40 and increases by about 1 m/s every decade; cfPWV > 10 m/s is considered the threshold for vascular stiffening. Aortic stiffness leads to increased systolic blood pressure, decreased diastolic blood pressure, increased pulse pressure, and increased left ventricular load and stroke risk. PWV is determined by both arterial wall structure (collagen/elastin ratio, AGEs cross-linking) and function (smooth muscle tone, NO-dependent relaxation) and is a stronger predictor of hard endpoints than FMD (recommended by ESC/ESH guidelines).
- **"Vascular age" concept**: "Vascular age" calculated based on FMD, PWV, carotid intima-media thickness (IMT) and other indicators can be higher or lower than chronological age, and is the projection of "biological age" in the vascular system (complementary to the apparent clock of 01-foundations/02-aging-clocks.md).

**Clinical Implications**: Regular measurement of FMD/cfPWV after the age of 40 is equivalent to an "annual checkup" of the vascular system. An increase in FMD or a decrease in PWV after intervention is direct evidence of NO pathway repair.

## 7.2 Comparison of NO supplement strategies: from drugs to lifestyle

| Strategy | NO Mode of Action | Speed of Action | Level of Evidence | Remarks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nitrates (nitroglycerin) | Directly provide exogenous NO | Minute level | A (symptom relief) | Tachyphylaxis, only used in the acute phase of angina |
| PDE5 inhibitor (sildenafil) | Inhibits cGMP degradation, amplifies endogenous NO | 30–60 minutes | A (ED/pulmonary hypertension) | Cannot increase NO production, requires endogenous NO base |
| Dietary nitrates (beetroot/leafy greens) | Nitrate → Nitrite → NO bypass | 2–3 hours | A/B (blood pressure lowering, athletic performance) | Safe, sustainable, better than supplements |
| L-arginine/L-citrulline | Provides eNOS substrate | Hours | B/C (mixed results) | Low oral bioavailability, controversial |
| Aerobic exercise | Shear stress → eNOS activation + expression ↑ | Weeks (chronic adaptation) | A (RCT meta) | The most comprehensive means of NO repair |
| Statins | Upregulate eNOS, inhibit oxidative stress | Several weeks | A | Prescription required for dyslipidemia |
| Antioxidant (VC/VE) | Scavenge O2⁻, reduce NO quenching | Acutely effective | B/C (no long-term benefit) | Routine supplementation not recommended |

**Core Principle**: Repair of endogenous NO pathways takes precedence over exogenous NO supplementation. Exercise, dietary nitrates, weight loss, and smoking cessation are strategies for "treating the root cause"; nitroglycerin and PDE5 inhibitors are tools for "treating the symptoms."

## 8. Open questions

1. **Precision Repair of eNOS Uncoupling**: How to maintain the BH4/BH2 ratio long-term without inhibiting eNOS itself? Can oral BH4 precursor or GTPCH-I upregulation strategies translate into clinical benefit?
2. **The "dual-sidedness" of NO**: How to define the safety window for the pro-inflammatory/carcinogenic effects of high concentrations of NO and peroxynitrite in the inflammatory microenvironment?
3. **FMD as a surrogate endpoint**: Does improvement in FMD reliably predict hard endpoints (MI/stroke)? The latest meta-analysis shows that its independent predictive value is moderate.
4. **The mechanism of traditional Chinese medicine intervention is blank**: The evidence for eNOS regulation of most traditional Chinese medicine ingredients remains at the cell/animal level, lacking standardized preparations and RCTs.
5. **Endothelial Progenitor Cell Therapy**: Whether NO-dependent EPC mobilization can become a clinical tool for vascular repair is still in early trials.
