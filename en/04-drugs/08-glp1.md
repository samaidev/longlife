# GLP-1 Receptor Agonists: From Glucose-Lowering Drugs to Anti-Aging Stars

> **Central view:** Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) are a drug family targeting the GLP-1 receptor. They promote glucose-dependent insulin secretion, suppress glucagon, slow gastric emptying, and suppress central appetite. Semaglutide 2.4 mg produced 15–17% weight loss in the STEP trials and reduced major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) by 20% in overweight/obese patients with cardiovascular disease in SELECT—elevating it from a “glucose-lowering drug” to a central tool in contemporary anti-aging and metabolic medicine. Through weight loss, improved insulin resistance, anti-inflammatory effects, and improved endothelial function, GLP-1RAs reshape several metabolic-aging phenotypes. However, debates such as “does weight loss equal anti-aging?” and “what is the cost of lean-mass loss?” mean the “anti-aging star” label still requires evidence on a decade-long timescale. This chapter reviews GLP-1 physiology, the evolution of the drug family, full molecular mechanisms, evidence grades, adverse effects, and intersections with the NO/NAD+ longevity network.

## 0. From Lizard Saliva to the World’s Top-Selling Drug

The origins of GLP-1RAs are dramatic. In the 1990s, researchers isolated exendin-4 from the saliva of the **Gila monster**—a peptide with about 53% sequence homology to human GLP-1 but a much longer half-life (about 30 minutes versus 2 minutes for human GLP-1). This discovery led to the first GLP-1RA, **exenatide**, approved by the FDA in 2005 for type 2 diabetes. Drug design has continued to evolve:

| Drug | Molecular design | Half-life | Administration | Year approved |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exenatide | Exendin-4 (lizard peptide), subcutaneous injection | About 2.4 h | Twice daily | 2005 |
| Liraglutide | Human GLP-1 with a fatty-acid chain (albumin binding prolongs half-life) | About 13 h | Once daily | 2010 |
| Dulaglutide | GLP-1 fused to an IgG4 Fc fragment | About 5 days | Once weekly | 2014 |
| Semaglutide | Human GLP-1 with several amino-acid substitutions plus fatty-acid side chain | About 1 week | Once weekly/oral | 2017 (subcutaneous) |
| Tirzepatide | Dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist (single molecule) | About 5 days | Once weekly | 2022 |
| Oral semaglutide | Semaglutide + SNAC absorption enhancer | About 1 week | Daily oral | 2019 |

**Why can’t GLP-1 itself be used as a drug?** Endogenous human GLP-1 has a half-life of only about two minutes and is rapidly degraded by dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4). Liraglutide’s C16 fatty-acid chain enables non-covalent binding to albumin in the body, protecting it from DPP-4 degradation and extending its half-life to 13 hours. Semaglutide further resists DPP-4 through two amino-acid substitutions (Ala8→Aib and fatty-acid acylation at Lys26) and has greater albumin affinity, enabling once-weekly dosing.

**Market scale**: GLP-1RAs have become one of the world’s best-selling drug classes. Global semaglutide sales were about US$20 billion in 2023, and its manufacturer Novo Nordisk briefly reached a market capitalization exceeding that of all European pharmaceutical companies combined. The transformation of GLP-1RAs from glucose-lowering drugs into weight-loss and cardiovascular drugs was named one of *Science*’s top ten scientific breakthroughs of 2023.

## 1. GLP-1 Physiology: The Molecular Basis of the Incretin System

### 1.1 GLP-1 secretion and metabolism

GLP-1 is a core member of the **incretin** family. After eating, nutrients—especially glucose and fat—stimulate **intestinal L cells**, found mainly in the ileum and colon and to a lesser extent in the duodenum and jejunum, to secrete GLP-1. Secretion occurs through two routes: ① nutrients directly enter L cells through SGLT1 and trigger secretion; ② vagal reflexes and neurotransmitters such as acetylcholine also contribute. The GLP-1 gene (GCG) encodes proglucagon, which is processed differently by tissue: pancreatic alpha cells produce glucagon, while intestinal L cells cleave proglucagon with PC1/3 to generate GLP-1(7-36) amide, the main active form.

```text
Eating → intestinal L cells → proglucagon → PC1/3 cleavage → GLP-1(7-36) amide (active)
                                                               │
                                    ┌──────────────────────────┤
                                    ▼                          ▼
                             binds GLP-1 receptor          degraded by DPP-4
                             (incretin effect)       → GLP-1(9-36) (inactive/antagonistic)
                                                    half-life only about 2 minutes
```

After secretion from intestinal L cells, most GLP-1 enters the liver through the portal vein; only a fraction reaches the systemic circulation. This is why the physiological post-meal rise in GLP-1 is limited (peak about 10–20 pmol/L) and its effects rely mainly on **local/paracrine signaling and vagus-mediated gut–brain-axis signals**.

### 1.2 Distribution of the GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R)

GLP-1R is a class B G-protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) that couples to Gαs upon activation and raises intracellular cAMP. Its distribution extends well beyond the pancreatic islets:

| Tissue | GLP-1R expression | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Pancreatic β cells | High | Glucose-dependent insulin secretion (incretin effect) |
| Pancreatic α cells | Present | Suppresses glucagon secretion (glucose-dependent) |
| Hypothalamus (arcuate and paraventricular nuclei) | Present | Activates POMC/CART neurons and inhibits AgRP neurons → appetite suppression and satiety |
| Brainstem (nucleus of the solitary tract, dorsal motor nucleus of vagus) | Present | Nausea/vomiting signals and gastrointestinal motility regulation |
| Stomach | Present | Delays gastric emptying |
| Heart (myocardium, vascular endothelium) | Low | Cardioprotection and vasodilation |
| Vascular smooth muscle/endothelium | Present | Vasodilation and anti-inflammatory action |
| Liver/adipose tissue | Low | Improves insulin sensitivity in adipose tissue |
| Immune cells (macrophages, T cells) | Present | Anti-inflammatory effects and promotion of M2 polarization |
| Kidneys | Present | Natriuresis and regulation of renal blood flow |

**Glucose dependence** is key to GLP-1RAs’ better safety profile than secretagogues such as sulfonylureas: when blood glucose is normal, GLP-1R activation has little insulin-stimulating effect; it substantially stimulates β cells only when glucose is elevated. Thus GLP-1RAs rarely cause hypoglycemia unless combined with sulfonylureas or insulin.

### 1.3 The incretin effect

The classic “incretin effect” is the finding that oral glucose induces more insulin secretion than the same amount of intravenous glucose. About 50–70% of this difference is mediated jointly by GLP-1 and glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP). The effect is markedly reduced in people with type 2 diabetes (partly because of β-cell GLP-1R downregulation and signaling defects), whereas exogenous GLP-1RAs at pharmacological concentrations can restore and even exceed the physiological incretin effect.

## 2. Full Molecular Mechanisms of GLP-1RAs

### 2.1 Insulin secretion: the principal glucose-dependent secretory pathway

The complete signaling chain after GLP-1RA activation of GLP-1R in β cells is:

```text
GLP-1RA → GLP-1R (Gαs GPCR)
   │
   ▼
adenylyl cyclase → cAMP↑ → PKA activation
   │
   ├─→ phosphorylates/inhibits K_ATP channels (SUR1/Kir6.2) → membrane depolarization
   │        → voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open → Ca²⁺ influx
   │        → insulin-granule exocytosis (synergizes with glucose stimulation)
   │
   ├─→ Epac2 (direct cAMP effector) → activates RAP1 → enhances insulin-granule mobilization and exocytosis
   │
   ├─→ phosphorylates CREB → insulin-gene transcription↑ → β-cell survival (anti-apoptotic)
   │        → activates PKA → Bcl-2↑ and Bax↓
   │
   └─→ activates PI3K/Akt → β-cell proliferation and survival (animal models)
```

**Synergy with glucose**: Glucose enters β cells through GLUT2 and increases the ATP/ADP ratio through glycolysis, directly inhibiting K_ATP channels and causing depolarization. Through cAMP/PKA, GLP-1RAs make K_ATP channels more sensitive to ATP, amplifying glucose-induced insulin secretion. This is the molecular basis of “glucose dependence”: when glucose is normal, K_ATP channels are already open and cAMP’s inhibitory effect is counterbalanced; when glucose is elevated, K_ATP channels tend to close and cAMP accelerates the process.

### 2.2 Appetite and body weight: the central nervous system hub

The main weight-loss target of GLP-1RAs is the **hypothalamic arcuate nucleus (ARC)**. GLP-1RAs reach the hypothalamus by two routes: ① crossing the blood–brain barrier (long-half-life GLP-1RAs can cross it); ② vagus-mediated signaling (intestinal GLP-1R activation → vagal afferent signals → nucleus of the solitary tract → hypothalamus).

```text
GLP-1RA → GLP-1R in the hypothalamic arcuate nucleus
   │
   ├─→ activates POMC/CART neurons (melanocortin system)
   │        → POMC processing → α-MSH → MC4R activation → appetite suppression, energy expenditure↑
   │
   └─→ inhibits AgRP/NPY neurons (orexigenic system)
            → AgRP release↓ → MC3/4R antagonism relieved → appetite suppression
   │
   ▼
food intake↓ + delayed gastric emptying (vagus→stomach) + reward-system modulation (nucleus accumbens)
   │
   ▼
caloric intake↓ → negative energy balance → adipose tissue↓ → body weight↓
```

Preclinical studies also show that GLP-1RAs modulate the **mesolimbic reward system** (dopaminergic pathways in the nucleus accumbens), reducing preference for and “craving” of high-fat, high-sugar foods. This may explain why weight loss exceeds what would be expected from delayed gastric emptying alone. fMRI studies suggest that in semaglutide users, reward-related brain regions (striatum and orbitofrontal cortex) respond less to food images.

### 2.3 Cardiovascular effects: integration from anti-inflammation to endothelial protection

GLP-1RA cardiovascular benefits involve multiple targets:

```text
GLP-1RA → GLP-1R activation
   │
   ├─→ vascular endothelium: PI3K/Akt → eNOS-Ser1177 phosphorylation → NO↑ → vasodilation
   ├─→ vascular smooth muscle: inhibits proliferation/migration (cGMP pathway), anti-atherosclerotic
   ├─→ macrophages: NF-κB inhibition → M1→M2 polarization → IL-6/CRP↓, IL-10↑
   ├─→ myocardium: PKA → anti-apoptosis (Bcl-2↑/Bax↓), protection from ischemia-reperfusion injury
   ├─→ sympathetic nervous system: reduced sympathetic activity, modest blood-pressure reduction
   └─→ indirect: weight loss/insulin-resistance improvement/lipid lowering → metabolic cardiovascular risk↓
```

Mechanistic analyses of SELECT suggest that semaglutide’s MACE benefit is **independent of the degree of weight loss**: risk reduction remained significant even in subgroups with modest weight loss, suggesting direct cardiovascular protection beyond weight loss (e.g., anti-inflammatory and endothelial effects). This resembles the logic for SGLT2 inhibitors—“cardioprotection independent of glucose lowering”—discussed in 02-vascular/03-metabolic-aging.md.

### 2.4 Inflammation and SASP: a potential anti-aging/anti-inflammatory connection

GLP-1RA-mediated suppression of inflammation may directly connect to the aging hallmarks of “chronic inflammation (inflammaging)” and the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP). Mechanistic studies indicate:

- **NF-κB inhibition**: GLP-1R activation inhibits IκB degradation, blocks NF-κB nuclear translocation, and downregulates transcription of pro-inflammatory factors such as TNF-α, IL-6, and MCP-1.
- **Macrophage polarization**: Promotes the anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype and suppresses the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype (preclinical studies).
- **Inflammatory markers fall**: In SELECT, hsCRP declined significantly in the semaglutide group versus placebo (about 40–50%); part of the decline was independent of weight change.
- **Direct effect on cellular senescence**: This remains at an early exploratory stage. Mechanistic studies suggest GLP-1R activation may inhibit senescence-related pathways through AMPK/mTOR, but there is no definitive in-vivo evidence that GLP-1RAs directly clear or reprogram senescent cells (senolytic/senomorphic effects). This differs from the SASP-downregulating anti-aging mechanism described for Rentosertib in 04-drugs/01-rentosertib.md.

## 3. Weight Loss and Metabolic Effects: Clinical Evidence

### 3.1 The STEP trials: a milestone in semaglutide weight loss

**STEP 1** (2021, *NEJM*) enrolled 1,961 non-diabetic adults with overweight/obesity. Semaglutide 2.4 mg once weekly plus lifestyle intervention produced mean weight loss of **14.9%** at 68 weeks (versus 2.4% with placebo); about 83% of participants lost at least 5% of body weight. **STEP 2** achieved about 9.6% weight loss in people with type 2 diabetes and obesity. **STEP 3**, combined with intensive behavioral intervention, achieved weight loss of about **16%**. **STEP 4** showed rapid weight regain after stopping treatment (see below). The STEP program established a key fact: **semaglutide’s 15–17% weight loss approaches the effect of bariatric surgery** and far exceeds that of earlier drugs.

### 3.2 Tirzepatide: stronger weight loss with a dual target

**SURMOUNT-1** (2022, *NEJM*) enrolled 2,539 non-diabetic people with overweight/obesity. Tirzepatide (dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist) at 15 mg produced mean weight loss of **20.9%** at 72 weeks (versus 3.1% with placebo). Its greater weight-loss effect than single-target GLP-1RAs may involve GIP’s synergistic effects on adipose tissue and the central nervous system. **SURMOUNT-4** also showed rebound after discontinuation, although somewhat less than with semaglutide.

### 3.3 Glucose-lowering effects

GLP-1RAs reduce HbA1c by about **1.0–2.0%** in type 2 diabetes, depending on baseline and dose, outperforming most oral glucose-lowering drugs. The SUSTAIN semaglutide program showed HbA1c reductions of about 1.5–1.8% and fasting-glucose reductions of about 2–3 mmol/L. **Mechanisms**: ① glucose-dependent insulin secretion (see 2.1); ② glucagon suppression (inhibition of α-cell secretion); ③ delayed gastric emptying (flattens the post-meal glucose curve); ④ improved insulin sensitivity through weight loss (long-term effect).

### 3.4 Cardiovascular benefits

| Trial | Drug/dose | Population | Main result |
|---|---|---|---|
| LEADER (2016) | Liraglutide 1.8 mg | 9,340 high-risk people with T2DM | MACE reduced 13% (HR 0.87) |
| SUSTAIN-6 (2016) | Semaglutide 0.5/1.0 mg | 3,297 high-risk people with T2DM | MACE reduced 26% (HR 0.74) |
| REWIND (2019) | Dulaglutide 1.5 mg | 9,901 people with T2DM | MACE reduced 12% (HR 0.88) |
| SELECT (2023) | Semaglutide 2.4 mg | 17,604 people with overweight/obesity and cardiovascular disease, without diabetes | MACE reduced 20% (HR 0.80); weight loss 9.4% |

**SELECT** is key evidence in the GLP-1RA anti-aging narrative. It was the first trial to show significant reduction in cardiovascular events with GLP-1RA treatment in a **non-diabetic population**. Among 17,604 people with overweight/obesity (BMI≥27) and cardiovascular disease but no diabetes, semaglutide 2.4 mg reduced MACE (cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, nonfatal stroke) by 20% over a mean 3.3 years, with consistent results across subgroups. Notably, cardiovascular death alone did not decline significantly (it did not reach independent significance); benefit was driven mainly by nonfatal myocardial infarction and stroke.

### 3.5 Kidney benefits: the FLOW trial

**FLOW** (2024) was the first trial specifically designed to verify renal outcomes with a GLP-1RA. Among 3,533 people with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease, semaglutide 1.0 mg reduced the primary composite kidney endpoint (≥50% decline in kidney function, end-stage kidney disease, or renal/cardiovascular death) by **24%** (HR 0.76). Mechanisms include improved hemodynamics (reduced glomerular hyperfiltration), anti-inflammatory effects, and antifibrotic effects.

## 4. GLP-1RAs in Aging Research: Does Weight Loss Equal Anti-Aging?

### 4.1 Effects on aging biomarkers and healthspan

Current evidence for GLP-1RAs across aging hallmarks:

| Aging hallmark (López-Otín 2023) | GLP-1RA effect | Evidence grade |
|---|---|---|
| Chronic inflammation (inflammaging) | hsCRP and IL-6 decline | B (RCT secondary endpoints) |
| Mitochondrial dysfunction | May improve through AMPK/PGC-1α | D (mechanistic hypothesis/animal) |
| Nutrient sensing (mTOR/AMPK) | Partly mimics caloric restriction through AMPK activation | C (mechanistic studies) |
| Cellular senescence/SASP | Indirectly (weight loss/anti-inflammation); little direct evidence | D (early stage) |
| Epigenetics | No direct data yet | Undetermined |
| Telomeres | No direct data | Undetermined |
| Proteostasis | May improve (weight loss/glucose lowering) | D |
| Stem-cell exhaustion | No direct data | Undetermined |

**GLP-1 signaling and animal longevity**: In *C. elegans*, reduced gut-derived insulin-like signaling (the insulin/IGF-1-like pathway, a related signaling family) is associated with lifespan extension—but this concerns the **insulin/IGF-1 pathway**, not GLP-1 itself. Evidence that GLP-1 receptor signaling directly extends animal lifespan remains insufficient. **Potential as a caloric-restriction (CR) mimetic**: By reducing caloric intake, activating AMPK, and lowering insulin/IGF-1 signaling, GLP-1RAs reproduce some metabolic effects of caloric restriction (see the discussion of CR in 02-vascular/03-metabolic-aging.md).

**Key distinction**: GLP-1RA weight loss improves **metabolic health and cardiovascular prognosis**, which are strongly related to **healthspan**, but this does **not** equal lifespan extension. There is currently no evidence that GLP-1RAs extend lifespan itself in humans or animals. Healthspan improvements occur by reducing obesity-associated comorbidities (cardiovascular disease, diabetes, fatty liver, etc.), rather than by directly acting on molecular clocks of aging.

### 4.2 Interpreting the SELECT trial as “anti-aging” evidence—and its limitations

Some media have described SELECT as “evidence for GLP-1RA anti-aging,” but caution is warranted:

- **Positive**: A 20% reduction in MACE among non-diabetic people shows benefits beyond glucose lowering and supports use in high-risk people with obesity even without diabetes—an attribute of “primary prevention of metabolic disease.”
- **Limitations**: ① The endpoint was cardiovascular events, not lifespan or aging biomarkers; ② cardiovascular death alone did not decline significantly; ③ follow-up was only 3.3 years, far too short to assess long-term anti-aging effects; ④ the population already had cardiovascular disease, so results cannot be generalized to healthy older adults.
- **Observational-cohort signals**: Some electronic-health-record studies report lower all-cause mortality among semaglutide users, but substantial confounding by indication (users are more likely to receive medical monitoring) prevents causal inference.

## 5. Adverse Effects, Muscle Loss, and Rebound after Stopping: The “Cost” of GLP-1RAs

### 5.1 Gastrointestinal adverse effects

The most common adverse effects are **nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and constipation**, especially early in treatment; most improve over time or with dose titration. In STEP trials, about 20–40% of participants experienced gastrointestinal events, and about 4–7% withdrew because of intolerance. Mechanisms include GLP-1R activation in the brainstem (area postrema and nucleus of the solitary tract), triggering nausea/vomiting signals, as well as delayed gastric emptying.

### 5.2 Muscle loss (sarcopenia) debate

This is the risk of greatest concern for anti-aging use in older adults. During weight loss, the proportion of lost weight that is lean mass is about **20–40%** (i.e., of every 10 kg lost, 2–4 kg is lean mass); losses are greater in older people and with larger weight reductions. This means:

- GLP-1RA weight loss can worsen **sarcopenic obesity**—a metabolically fragile state with little muscle and excess fat.
- Older adults already lose muscle with age (about 3–8% per decade); adding GLP-1RA-related weight loss may increase frailty risk.
- **Countermeasures**: High-protein intake (≥1.2–1.5 g/kg/day) plus resistance training is standard advice. In anti-aging settings, “lose fat while preserving muscle” must be an essential adjunct.

### 5.3 Other rare but important adverse effects

- **Gallbladder disease**: Slightly increased risk of cholecystitis and gallstones (bile stasis associated with rapid weight loss); gallbladder events increased in SELECT.
- **Pancreatitis**: A risk signal exists, but causality is unclear; avoid or use caution in people with a history of pancreatitis.
- **Thyroid C-cell tumors**: GLP-1R agonists cause thyroid C-cell hyperplasia and medullary carcinoma in rodents, but GLP-1R expression on human C cells is very low and epidemiology has not confirmed increased human risk.
- **Rare worsening of retinopathy**: Rapid glucose reduction may worsen proliferative retinopathy (mainly in T2DM); monitoring is needed.
- **Muscle loss and bone density**: Some studies suggest weight loss is accompanied by lower bone density; this may be due to weight loss itself rather than a drug-specific effect.

### 5.4 Rebound after discontinuation

**STEP 4** showed that after semaglutide discontinuation, about two-thirds of the lost weight returned within one year (an original 17.4% reduction dwindled to only about 5% net benefit). **SURMOUNT-4** also showed rebound after stopping tirzepatide. Mechanistically, appetite suppression is lifted when GLP-1RA is stopped; without behavioral interventions (exercise and diet) as an “anchor,” negative energy balance is hard to maintain. This suggests **GLP-1RAs are a “tool,” not a “cure”**—long-term maintenance requires continued medication or a handoff to sustained behavioral interventions.

## 6. Connections to the NO/NAD+ Longevity Network

### 6.1 GLP-1RA → eNOS → NO pathway

GLP-1R is expressed in vascular endothelial cells. The vascular-protective signaling chain after activation is:

```text
GLP-1RA → endothelial GLP-1R (Gαs)
   │
   ▼
cAMP↑ → PKA/Epac → PI3K/Akt
   │
   ▼
eNOS-Ser1177 phosphorylation (activation) + stabilization by eNOS–heat-shock protein 90 (HSP90) binding
   │
   ▼
NO production↑ → diffuses to smooth muscle → soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC)
   │
   ▼
cGMP↑ → PKG → myosin-light-chain phosphatase activation → vasodilation
   │
   └─→ inhibits platelet aggregation, leukocyte adhesion, and oxidative stress
```

**Evidence grading**: Evidence that endothelial eNOS activation increases NO is mainly **preclinical/mechanistic** (isolated vascular rings and cell models). Results of acute GLP-1 infusion on human flow-mediated dilation (FMD) are **mixed**. Some studies show improved endothelial function with long-term GLP-1RA treatment, but this may partly result indirectly from weight loss, glucose lowering, and lipid improvement rather than direct eNOS activation. Metabolites such as GLP-1(9-36) may also exert vascular protection independently (mechanistic studies). Overall: **mechanisms support a connection between GLP-1RAs and the NO pathway, but direct human evidence is moderate to weak.**

### 6.2 Integration with the metabolic–vascular–NO axis

GLP-1RA weight loss improves insulin resistance → restores endothelial PI3K/Akt→eNOS signaling (this pathway is impaired in insulin resistance; see “insulin resistance↔NO deficiency” in 02-vascular/03-metabolic-aging.md) → NO bioavailability rises. Thus the NO benefit of GLP-1RAs may be **indirect but systemic**: they do not directly supply NO, but repair metabolic damage to endothelial NO signaling. This is complementary in logic to metformin/AMPK activation of eNOS (see 04-drugs/07-metformin-ampk.md)—one repairs metabolism, the other directly modulates AMPK/eNOS.

### 6.3 Intersection with NAD+/SIRT1

GLP-1RAs activate AMPK, which intersects closely with SIRT1 (AMPK promotes NAD+ salvage synthesis and activates SIRT1); weight loss also reduces oxidative stress and PARP-mediated NAD+ consumption. However, these links in the GLP-1RA setting are mostly **inferences** and lack direct data. Combining GLP-1RAs with NAD+ precursors (NMN/NR) is an attractive anti-aging hypothesis but remains entirely unvalidated.

### 6.4 Dialogue with the traditional Chinese medicine concept of “boosting qi and activating blood”

The “boost qi and activate blood” herbs discussed in this knowledge base (astragalus, danshen, chuanxiong, etc.) provide cardiovascular protection by improving blood flow and NO signaling and reducing inflammation. Core GLP-1RA benefits (anti-inflammatory effects, endothelial improvement, and anti-atherosclerosis) overlap substantially with the effects attributed to “activating blood” at the outcome level, but the mechanisms differ greatly (specific GPCR targeting versus multi-component, multi-target action). In real-world settings, the two might form a complementary framework of “modern drugs + traditional supportive care,” but the strength of evidence for herbal anti-aging is far below the GLP-1RA RCT evidence base.

## 7. Similarities and Differences from Lifestyle Interventions: Appetite Suppression vs. Restriction

GLP-1RA weight loss resembles **caloric restriction (CR)/fasting** at the surface level—both reduce caloric intake—but differs substantially at the molecular level:

| Dimension | GLP-1RA | Caloric restriction/fasting |
|---|---|---|
| How caloric intake falls | Central appetite suppression + delayed gastric emptying | Voluntary behavioral restriction |
| AMPK activation | Moderate (direct drug effect + indirect weight-loss effect) | Strong (direct response to energy scarcity) |
| mTORC1 inhibition | Indirect (less nutrition) | Direct (amino-acid/energy scarcity) |
| Autophagy induction | Limited evidence | Strong during fasting |
| Ketones/metabolic flexibility | Not actively induced | Marked during fasting |
| Muscle preservation | Weight loss includes lean-mass loss | Can be preserved with exercise + protein |
| Sustainability | Rebound after stopping | Requires long-term behavioral maintenance |

Key point: **GLP-1RAs are “chemical restriction,” but do not fully activate the energy-deficit signals of restriction (strong AMPK activation and initiation of autophagy).** They produce highly effective weight loss, but whether “metabolic improvement from weight loss” is equivalent to the molecular anti-aging signals of restriction remains uncertain. The ideal scenario combines them: GLP-1RA helps establish negative energy balance, while behavioral interventions (resistance training and protein intake) preserve muscle and restriction/exercise maintain metabolic flexibility.

## 8. Intervention Directions and Practical Recommendations

### 8.1 Current evidence-supported populations

- **Clear benefit**: Type 2 diabetes (glucose, cardiovascular, and kidney outcomes); overweight/obesity (BMI≥30, or ≥27 with metabolic/cardiovascular disease); overweight/obesity with cardiovascular disease (SELECT evidence).
- **Position in anti-aging**: Best-supported as a “metabolic-health repair tool” for obesity-driven metabolic aging, cardiovascular risk, and inflammatory states. It should not be a universal anti-aging drug for healthy older adults.

### 8.2 Practical steps to lose fat while preserving muscle

```text
During GLP-1RA weight loss
   ├─→ high-protein diet (≥1.2–1.5 g/kg/day) → counter lean-mass loss
   ├─→ resistance training (2–3 times/week) → muscle-anabolic stimulus + insulin sensitivity
   ├─→ gradual dose titration → fewer gastrointestinal intolerances
   ├─→ monitor muscle mass (DEXA/bioelectrical impedance), especially in older adults
   └─→ behavioral support when stopping → prevent rebound
```

### 8.3 Prospective combination strategies (unvalidated hypotheses)

- GLP-1RA + exercise (strongest rationale; exercise preserves muscle and enhances metabolic benefits)
- GLP-1RA + metformin (complementary: one addresses energy intake, the other cellular energy sensing through AMPK)
- GLP-1RA + protein/leucine supplementation (counter sarcopenia)
- GLP-1RA + NMN/NR (NAD+ hypothesis, unvalidated)

## 9. Open Questions and Controversies

1. **Does weight loss equal anti-aging?** This is the central debate. GLP-1RAs improve metabolic health and cardiovascular prognosis (relevant to healthspan), but there is no evidence that they extend lifespan itself. “Repairing obesity-driven metabolic disease” and “directly slowing aging” are different intervention logics: the former is well supported; the latter remains hypothetical.
2. **The cost of lean-mass loss**: In older adults, muscle and bone loss accompanying weight reduction could offset cardiovascular gains. The relationships between degree and speed of weight loss and muscle loss—and whether exercise can fully reverse it—remain unresolved.
3. **Rebound and lifelong treatment**: Some metabolic benefits recede after GLP-1RA discontinuation. This raises the practical question of whether “lifelong treatment” is needed and what decade-scale use does to muscle, kidneys, thyroid, and cancer risk.
4. **Direct effects on cellular senescence**: Are GLP-1RAs merely metabolic/anti-inflammatory tools, or can they also directly clear or reprogram senescent cells (senolytic/senomorphic effects)? Current indirect evidence (anti-inflammation, AMPK activation) suggests possible interaction with aging, but direct evidence is nearly absent.
5. **GLP-1RAs and mental health**: Reports have raised a rare signal for suicidal ideation (the EMA evaluation did not confirm causality). Effects of appetite suppression and reward-system modulation on depression and eating disorders should be monitored.
6. **Equity and access**: The high price and need for long-term use of GLP-1RAs as “anti-aging drugs,” and expansion of indications to healthy populations, raise ethical and resource-allocation questions: should these be “lifestyle drugs” or treatments for disease?
