# 09 · The Immortality Quest

[English index](../README.md) · [Chinese source](../../09-immortality/README.md)

> This topic examines possible “immortality drug” breakthroughs as scientific questions rather than promises. It is organized around definitions, candidates, mechanisms, and research roadmaps. The source describes this as a synthesis connected to the other areas of the knowledge base.

## Contents

- [01 · Scientific Definition and Feasibility Framework](../../09-immortality/01-immortality-framework.md) — The meaning of biological immortality (mortality not increasing with age), cultural immortality, and medical life extension; aging theories; lifespan-limit debates; and the idea of multi-target engineering.
- [02 · Six Candidate Breakthrough Paths: A Comparison](../../09-immortality/02-breakthrough-candidates.md) — A six-dimension assessment framework (depth of reversal, evidence, clinical progress, safety, scalability, and alignment with the knowledge base’s themes); candidates discussed include epigenetic reprogramming, senolytics, mTOR/AMPK, telomerase, NAD⁺/mitochondria, gene therapy, and chromatin.
- [03 · Mechanisms of Death and Lifespan Limits](../../09-immortality/03-death-mechanism.md) — Accidental, programmed, and age-related death; classical evolutionary theories of aging; and debates about human lifespan limits.
- [04 · Research and Development Roadmap](../../09-immortality/04-roadmap.md) — A proposed staged roadmap, personal longevity strategies, and open questions concerning access, identity, and resource allocation.

## Key conclusions stated in the Chinese source

The source argues that an “immortality drug” would more plausibly mean a combination of interventions that alter mortality risk over time than a single drug. It presents epigenetic reprogramming as a candidate research direction and makes time-sensitive claims about trial progress and biological-age reversal. These claims are not independently verified in this English index; consult primary publications and current trial registries. Biological immortality is not an established medical outcome.
