# Exercise Prescription: A Systematic Longevity Intervention Centered on Shear Stress

> Core idea: Exercise is the most well-evidenced and broadest-coverage "multi-target drug" among existing anti-aging interventions—it simultaneously acts on five major anti-aging hubs: vasculature (NO/shear stress), mitochondria (PGC-1α/mitophagy), telomeres (telomerase), epigenetics (DNA methylation clocks), and neuroscience (BDNF/hippocampal neurogenesis). Unlike single-target drugs, exercise has no "ceiling" before dose toxicity limits; instead, it has a clear dose-response curve and U-shaped relationship: insufficient is ineffective, optimal is best, excessive is harmful. This chapter provides a complete framework for evidence-based exercise prescription: type overview, systemic benefits, molecular mechanisms, evidence grading, and individualized execution plans.

## 1. Core Concept

**Exercise Prescription** refers to a systematically designed exercise program following the FITT principle (Frequency, Intensity, Time, Type), with the goal not to "move a bit" but to reach a dose threshold sufficient to trigger molecular adaptation. Exercise is the cornerstone intervention in anti-aging research for three reasons: ① It is the only physiological source of **shear stress**, which is the most powerful endogenous activation signal for the eNOS/NO pathway (see 02-vascular/01-no-cardiovascular.md); ② It is the most reliable upstream inducer of **PGC-1α mitochondrial biogenesis**, directly countering mitochondrial aging—a core hallmark of aging; ③ It forms a systemic network through hormones, myokines, metabolites, and epigenetic modifications, positively intersecting with at least 9 of the 12 hallmarks of aging (López-Otín 2023): genomic instability (DNA repair enzyme upregulation), telomere attrition (telomerase activation), epigenetic alterations (methylation clock reversal), loss of proteostasis (molecular chaperone HSP upregulation), mitochondrial dysfunction (biogenesis), cellular senescence (enhanced immune clearance), chronic inflammation (IL-6/CRP reduction), altered nutrient sensing (AMPK/mTOR rebalancing), and stem cell exhaustion (satellite cell activation).

The essential characteristic distinguishing exercise from nutritional supplementation and drugs: it is an intervention that simultaneously perturbs **mechanical signals, energy signals, and neural signals** in a single session—no oral molecule can replicate the physical shear of blood flow on vessels, the metabolic demands of skeletal muscle contraction, and the electrical activity of the central nervous system. This makes exercise an "upstream, holistic" intervention, while drugs are mostly "downstream, targeted" interventions.

## 2. Exercise Type Overview: Four Pillars and Their Anti-Aging Focus

| Type | Physiological Characteristics | Core Molecular Targets | Anti-Aging Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aerobic endurance (running/swimming/cycling) | Sustained moderate-high intensity, cardiopulmonary work | eNOS/NO, PGC-1α, mitochondrial density, capillarization | Cardiovascular, mitochondria, telomeres |
| Resistance strength (weights/resistance bands) | High tension, short duration, muscle hypertrophy | mTORC1, muscle satellite cells, IGF-1/Akt, Myostatin inhibition | Sarcopenia, bone density, metabolism |
| High-intensity interval HIIT | Repeated brief maximal efforts + recovery | AMPK, PGC-1α, calcium signaling (CaMK), peak shear stress | Most time-efficient VO₂max and mitochondrial stimulation |
| Flexibility/balance (yoga/tai chi/stretching) | Low load, joint mobility | Proprioception, HPA axis, inflammation (CRP reduction) | Fall prevention, functional preservation in aging, psychology |

Key concept: **Myokine**—cytokines secreted by skeletal muscle during contraction, the material basis for "exercise as an endocrine intervention." Among these, IL-6's role reverses during exercise: IL-6 released during acute exercise is anti-inflammatory (activates AMPK, promotes fat oxidation), distinctly different from pro-inflammatory IL-6 in chronic inflammation; other important myokines include irisin (promotes white fat browning), SPARC (inhibits colon cancer), BDNF (neuroprotection), etc. The essence of **exercise adaptation** is "repeated brief stress → supercompensation": gene expression upregulates during the recovery period after each training session, accumulating into functional and morphological changes.

## 3. Complete Molecular Mechanism Chain: How Exercise Simultaneously Activates Five Anti-Aging Hubs

### 3.1 Hub One: Shear Stress → eNOS → NO (Vascular Axis)

```text
Accelerated blood flow → endothelial shear stress (10-20 dyn/cm²)
   │
   ├→ Mechanosensory complex: PECAM-1 → VEGFR2 → VE-cadherin / integrin αvβ3
   │        ▼
   │     Src → PI3K → PIP3 → PDK1 → Akt(Thr308)
   │        ▼
   │     eNOS-Ser1177 phosphorylation (activation) + Thr495 dephosphorylation (disinhibition)
   │        ▼
   │     L-arginine + O₂ → NO → sGC → cGMP → PKG → vasodilation/anti-atherosclerosis/anti-thrombosis
   │
   └→ Chronic adaptation: eNOS gene expression↑ / SOD↑ / NF-κB inflammatory pathway↓ / antioxidant↑
        → "Shear stress memory": resting endothelial function baseline overall elevation
```

This is the first mechanistic layer of exercise's cardiovascular benefits. FMD transiently increases 30–60 minutes after acute exercise (NO-dependent, can be blocked by L-NAME); regular exercise forms **long-term endothelial function gains** through upregulating eNOS expression and antioxidant enzymes (SOD, catalase). Exercise causes structural vascular remodeling: improved flow-mediated dilation (FMD), reduced arterial stiffness (decreased pulse wave velocity PWV), and increased capillary density.

### 3.2 Hub Two: AMPK/PGC-1α → Mitochondrial Biogenesis (Energy Axis)

```text
Muscle contraction → ATP consumption → AMP/ATP ratio↑
   │
   ▼
AMPK activation (Thr172 phosphorylation)
   ├→ PGC-1α phosphorylation/deacetylation activation (SIRT1 assists deacetylation)
   ├→ PGC-1α expression↑ (CaMK via HDAC inhibition relief, p38 MAPK involvement)
   ▼
Nuclear PGC-1α cooperates with NRF1/2, ERRα
   ▼
TFAM upregulation → mitochondrial DNA replication → mitochondrial biogenesis
   ▼
Increased oxidative phosphorylation complexes, mitochondrial density↑, fatty acid oxidation↑
```

This is the core chain of "exercise reversing mitochondrial aging." Key regulation: SIRT1 enhances PGC-1α activity through deacetylation—**exercise converges directly with NAD⁺ supplementation interventions through the NAD⁺/SIRT1/PGC-1α axis (see relevant chapters in 07-interventions)**. AMPK also directly phosphorylates and activates mitophagy receptors (ULK1, BNIP3, FUNDC1), clearing damaged mitochondria—i.e., "exercise promotes mitochondrial quality control (MQC)": first clear the bad (autophagy), then build new (biogenesis), constituting true "mitochondrial renewal."

### 3.3 Hub Three: BDNF/Hippocampal Neurogenesis (Neural Axis)

Exercise upregulates brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) via two pathways: ① Metabolites and myokines produced by skeletal muscle (e.g., irisin) cross the blood-brain barrier to act on the hippocampus; ② β-hydroxybutyrate produced by the liver (ketone bodies rise during exercise) inhibits histone deacetylases HDAC2/3, relieving epigenetic repression of the BDNF promoter. BDNF activates downstream CREB via TrkB receptors, promotes hippocampal dentate gyrus neurogenesis, and enhances synaptic plasticity. The **FNDC5/irisin pathway** is a recent hotspot: exercise induces muscle FNDC5 expression, whose cleavage product irisin promotes hippocampal BDNF expression—a "muscle-brain axis." Epidemiologically, cardiorespiratory fitness levels in middle-aged and older adults are positively correlated with hippocampal volume and cognitive scores; RCTs show aerobic exercise increases hippocampal volume by approximately 2% annually (vs. ~1.4% atrophy in controls).

### 3.4 Hub Four: Telomerase Activation (Telomere Axis)

Telomere length (TL) is a biomarker of replicative senescence. Cross-sectional and prospective studies consistently show that regular exercisers have significantly longer peripheral blood leukocyte telomere length than sedentary individuals. Mechanisms: ① Exercise upregulates telomerase reverse transcriptase (TERT) activity—telomerase activity in peripheral blood mononuclear cells can increase within 24 hours after a single acute exercise bout; ② Exercise reduces oxidative stress and chronic inflammation (the two major drivers of telomere attrition); ③ Exercise upregulates Shelterin complex proteins (TRF1/TRF2). **Quantified anchor**: US CDC cohort studies show that compared to sedentary individuals, those meeting recommended exercise levels had telomere length differences equivalent to being approximately 9 years younger in biological age (reference data, with population variation); runner cohorts (running >30 minutes/day, ≥5 days/week) had significantly longer telomeres than age-matched non-runners, with a dose-response trend relative to running volume. Note: Telomere length measurement methodology (qPCR T/S ratio vs. single molecule) varies greatly, effect sizes fluctuate markedly across studies, and the evidence level is "strong association" rather than "definitive proof."

### 3.5 Hub Five: Epigenetic Modifications (Methylation Clock Axis)

Regular exercise is associated with deceleration of DNA methylation age (Horvath clock, PhenoAge, GrimAge). Mechanisms: ① Exercise alters DNMT and TET enzyme activity, affecting gene promoter methylation; ② Exercise upregulates histone acetyltransferases (altered HDAC activity), such as HDAC5 nuclear translocation regulating metabolic genes; ③ Exercise-induced metabolites (α-ketoglutarate, SAM) are substrates/cofactors for methylation reactions. **Note**: Current evidence is mostly cross-sectional associations and short-term interventions (weeks to months) showing methylation site changes; long-term RCTs validating exercise "reversing epigenetic age" are still ongoing, with evidence level at "mechanistic speculation + animal support" or above.

## 4. Systemic Benefits of Exercise: Evidence Overview Across Nine Systems

| System | Effect | Effect Size/Quantified Anchor | Evidence Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cardiovascular | CHD risk↓, blood pressure↓, FMD↑ | CVD risk reduction 30–40%; systolic BP reduction 5–10 mmHg | Definitive (RCT + meta-analysis) |
| Metabolic | Insulin sensitivity↑, blood glucose↓ | T2DM risk reduction ~40–58% (Diabetes Prevention Program DPP) | Definitive (RCT) |
| Neural | BDNF↑, hippocampal volume↑, cognitive risk↓ | Hippocampal annual growth ~2% (vs. control atrophy 1.4%) | Strong association (partial RCT) |
| Musculoskeletal | Muscle strength↑, muscle mass↑, bone density↑ | Resistance training 12 weeks: strength increase 20–40% | Definitive |
| Immune | NK cell activity↑, vaccine response↑ | NK cell mobilization 3–10 fold after acute exercise | Strong association |
| Mitochondria | Mitochondrial density↑, oxidative capacity↑ | Mitochondrial volume density increase 20–50% after aerobic training | Definitive (biopsy studies) |
| Telomeres | Telomerase activity↑, telomere length preservation | Telomerase activity increase after single exercise bout | Strong association |
| Inflammation | CRP, IL-6, TNF-α↓ | Regular exercise CRP reduction 15–30% | Strong association (partial RCT) |
| Epigenetics | Methylation age deceleration | Some cross-sectional studies show 2–9 year difference | Mechanistic speculation/animal support |

**Metabolic details**: The DPP study (NEJM 2002) is a milestone for exercise intervention in metabolism—lifestyle intervention (≥150 minutes/week moderate-intensity exercise + 7% weight loss) reduced diabetes incidence risk by 58% in high-risk populations, outperforming metformin's 31%; and this benefit persisted over 10-year follow-up (DPPOS). Mechanisms by which exercise improves insulin sensitivity: enhanced GLUT4 translocation, improved skeletal muscle lipid oxidation (reduced lipotoxicity), and myokines (IL-6/irisin) improving liver and adipose tissue insulin signaling.

**Immune details**: Regular moderate-intensity exercise enhances immune surveillance—NK cells are mobilized to peripheral blood within 30 minutes of acute exercise (increasing several-fold), with enhanced tissue homing 24 hours post-exercise; regular exercise in older adults improves influenza vaccine antibody response (RCT evidence: exercise group antibody titers significantly higher than control). The "open window hypothesis" suggests that upper respiratory infection risk transiently increases for several hours after a single exhaustive high-intensity exercise bout; therefore, immune benefits come from **regularity rather than single-session intensity**.

## 5. Evidence Grading

| Phenomenon | Evidence Level | Key Literature |
|---|---|---|
| Exercise reduces all-cause/cardiovascular mortality (dose-response) | Definitive (large prospective cohorts + meta-analysis) | Arem et al. JAMA Intern Med 2015 (n≈660,000) |
| Exercise prevents T2DM (DPP lifestyle intervention) | Definitive (RCT) | Knowler et al. NEJM 2002 |
| HIIT improves VO₂max superior to/equivalent to MICT (higher time efficiency) | Definitive (meta-analysis) | Milanović et al. Sports Med 2015; Weston et al. 2014 |
| Exercise improves FMD (endothelial function) | Definitive (meta-analysis) | Ashor et al. Atherosclerosis 2015 |
| Exercisers have longer telomeres | Strong association (observational; mechanistically supported by animal telomerase↑) | Cherkas et al. 2008; Ludlow et al. 2014 (review) |
| Exercise delays epigenetic clocks | Mechanistic speculation + partial animal support (mostly cross-sectional) | Multiple methylation studies, awaiting long-term RCTs |
| Antioxidant supplementation offsets exercise mitochondrial benefits | Animal support + human mechanistic studies (Ristow 2009 human experiment) | Ristow et al. PNAS 2009 |
| Excessive exercise (marathon-level) transiently elevates cardiac damage markers | Observational (transient cardiac troponin elevation, long-term outcomes unclear) | Multiple marathon cardiac biomarker studies |

**Grading notes**: All-cause mortality, diabetes, and cardiovascular endpoints are the areas with the strongest exercise evidence (dozens of RCTs + million-level cohorts); telomeres and epigenetic clocks remain at "strong association" due to measurement methodology and confounding (sedentary individuals often also smoke/are obese/have poor sleep); the exercise-mitochondria-ROS hormesis mechanism has solid mechanistic research but limited human evidence.

## 6. Clinical/Experimental Evidence Details

### 6.1 Dose-Response and U-Shaped Relationship

Arem et al. (JAMA Intern Med 2015, ~660,000 adult cohort) provide the classic exercise-mortality curve: compared to complete inactivity, **even minimal exercise (<half the recommended amount) reduces all-cause mortality risk by ~20%**; reaching 150 minutes/week of moderate intensity reduces risk by ~31%; at 3–5 times the recommended amount (450–750 minutes/week), benefits plateau (~39%), **not rising indefinitely with dose**. The right side of the U/inverted J-shaped curve (extreme doses) has more controversial evidence: marathon, triathlon, and other extreme endurance athletes have lifespans comparable to or even better than the general population ("long-lived athlete" studies show no clear harm), but acute cardiovascular event risk transiently increases during extreme exercise (exercise-induced sudden cardiac death annual incidence ~1/100,000–1/50,000, overwhelmingly in unscreened individuals with pre-existing heart disease). **Clinical interpretation: getting sedentary individuals "moving" yields the greatest marginal benefit; moderate is sufficient; no need to pursue extreme doses.**

### 6.2 HIIT vs. MICT Adaptation Comparison

| Dimension | HIIT (e.g., 4×4 min at 90% VO₂max) | MICT (e.g., 40 min at 65% VO₂max) |
|---|---|---|
| Single session duration | 20–30 minutes (including warm-up) | 40–60 minutes |
| VO₂max improvement (8–12 weeks) | ~8–10% (up to 15% in some studies) | ~5–8% |
| Mitochondrial oxidative enzyme activity (CS, COX) | Equivalent or slightly superior improvement | Improvement |
| Peak shear stress/FMD improvement | Higher (intermittent high blood flow) | Moderate |
| Time efficiency | ~2× efficiency (benefit per unit time) | Baseline |
| Adherence/applicable populations | Cardiovascular disease requires prior assessment | Broad |

Mechanistic differences: HIIT rapidly activates PGC-1α through **dual calcium signaling (CaMKII) + AMPK pathways** (low-intensity long-duration exercise relies more on the single AMPK pathway and substrate utilization), and the intermittent recovery periods create "reperfusion/shear stress peaks"; MICT has greater total work output and higher fat oxidation contribution. Conclusion: **Both improve health indicators; HIIT is the first choice for time-constrained individuals; MICT is the safety and adherence baseline.** Classic protocol: Wisløff 4×4 protocol (4 minutes at 85–95% HRmax × 4 sets, with 3-minute active recovery intervals) is equally effective in coronary artery disease and heart failure patients (RCT evidence).

### 6.3 Strength Training and Sarcopenia

Sarcopenia—age-related decline in muscle mass and function—begins with muscle loss of ~3–8% per decade from age 30, accelerating after 60, and is an important predictor of falls, disability, and all-cause mortality. Resistance training is the first-line intervention against sarcopenia:

- **Progressive overload**: Small load increases every 1–2 weeks (weight/reps/sets), continuously providing new muscle stimuli, driving mTORC1-S6K1 pathway activation, muscle satellite cell proliferation and differentiation, and muscle fiber cross-sectional area increase;
- **Protein intake synergy**: Older adults have "anabolic resistance"—muscle synthesis response to equivalent protein intake is weaker than in younger adults. Countermeasure: 25–30 g high-quality protein per meal (leucine ≥2.5 g/meal), supplementation within 2 hours post-exercise is optimal; total protein recommendation 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day (healthy older adults);
- **Evidence**: Resistance training for 12–24 weeks increases muscle strength by 20–40% and muscle mass by 1–2 kg in older adults (meta-analysis data), with effects not diminishing with age—**individuals over 80 can also achieve significant training adaptation**;
- **Relationship with aerobic exercise**: Resistance primarily targets muscle mass and strength, aerobic primarily targets cardiorespiratory fitness and mitochondria; the two have complementary mechanisms for improving insulin sensitivity, and concurrent training is recommended for older adult prescriptions (note: high-volume aerobic on the same day may slightly suppress resistance hypertrophic signaling; generally recommended to separate time slots or alternate days).

### 6.4 Exercise and Mitochondria: The mitohormesis Controversy

Ristow et al. (PNAS 2009) classic human experiment: healthy young males underwent 4 weeks of endurance training while supplementing vitamin C (1000 mg/day) + vitamin E (400 IU/day); their training-induced upregulation of mitochondrial biogenesis markers (PGC-1α, NRF1, TFAM expression) and endogenous antioxidant defense (SOD, GPx activity) was **significantly attenuated**—i.e., high-dose antioxidants "washed away" part of exercise's molecular benefits. This study proposed the **mitohormesis hypothesis**: the transient ROS increase produced by exercise (mitochondrial electron transport chain leakage) is a necessary "hormetic toxin" for triggering adaptive signaling—ROS activates AMPK, Nrf2, PGC-1α, which in turn upregulate antioxidant defense and mitochondrial function. **Practical implication: healthy individuals engaging in regular exercise need not routinely supplement high-dose antioxidants, which may blunt training adaptation; but this conclusion does not equate to "antioxidants are harmful," and its applicability in elderly/disease populations remains to be verified.** Subsequent animal studies (e.g., mice exercise + vitamin C supplementation) partially replicated and partially failed to replicate this effect; human evidence still centers on Ristow 2009 as the landmark study.

### 6.5 Exercise and NO: Quantitative Evidence

- FMD increases 30–60 minutes after a single aerobic exercise bout (NO-dependent, L-NAME can block);
- 12 weeks of HIIT significantly improves FMD and peak VO₂ in coronary artery disease, T2DM, and older adults (multiple RCTs); HIIT's FMD improvement magnitude can reach ~8.25% in systematic reviews (the largest effect size among exercise modalities);
- **Exercise vs. dietary nitrate**: Exercise is an "active" endogenous NO enhancer (shear stress → eNOS full-chain upregulation), while dietary nitrate (beetroot juice, etc.) uses the "non-enzymatic bypass" (nitrate → nitrite → NO); the two mechanisms are complementary and additive; but nitrate supplementation cannot replace exercise's shear stress effects (vascular remodeling, mitochondrial adaptation), see 02-vascular/03-metabolic-aging.md and the nitrate chapter;
- Strength training also activates eNOS (increased muscle blood flow), but evidence for endothelial function improvement is weaker than for aerobic/HIIT.

## 7. Connection to the NO / NAD⁺ / Longevity Network

**Exercise is the natural convergence point of the NO pathway and NAD⁺ pathway**:

```text
Exercise
 ├→ Shear stress → eNOS → NO ──→ Vascular health (see 02-vascular chapters)
 └→ Muscle contraction → AMP/ATP↑ → AMPK
       ├→ NAD⁺ consuming enzymes: muscle NAD⁺ pool is consumed during acute exercise
       ├→ NAMPT expression↑ → salvage synthesis pathway accelerated → NAD⁺ recovery/supercompensation
       │     ("exercise trains the NAD⁺ salvage pathway")
       └→ SIRT1 activation (NAD⁺-dependent) → PGC-1α deacetylation → mitochondrial biogenesis
             └→ SIRT3 (mitochondrial deacetylase) activation → antioxidant/metabolic enzyme activation
```

Mechanistic studies show: acute exercise transiently decreases skeletal muscle NAD⁺ (consumed by SIRT1/deacetylation reactions), followed by compensatory NAMPT upregulation and NAD⁺ supercompensation—this explains why regular exercisers' muscle NAD⁺ levels rise with training years, while sedentary aging individuals show declining NAMPT expression and NAD⁺ depletion. **Theoretical synergy between exercise and NAD⁺ supplementation (NMN/NR)**: Exercise "trains" the NAD⁺ synthesis machinery, NAD⁺ supplementation provides substrate—the two may be additive, but human RCT evidence remains limited, and there is controversy that NAD⁺ precursor supplementation during exercise may blunt AMPK signaling (excessively high NAD⁺ levels may create negative feedback). **Synergy between exercise and senolytics**: Exercise enhances immune clearance of senescent cells (NK cell mobilization, perforin expression↑), theoretically forming a "sweep + maintain" combination with senolytic drugs; exercise also reduces new senescent cell production by lowering chronic inflammation.

## 8. Intervention Directions and Actionable Recommendations (Evidence-Based Exercise Prescription)

**WHO 2020 Guideline Baseline**: ≥150–300 minutes/week moderate-intensity aerobic (or 75–150 minutes high-intensity), plus ≥2 days/week whole-body large muscle group resistance training; age 65+ add ≥3 days/week multicomponent balance/flexibility training. This library's advanced prescription incorporating NO/mitochondrial evidence:

| Component | Prescription | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Basic aerobic | 150 minutes/week moderate intensity (e.g., brisk walking, cycling), over 4–5 days | Ensure sustained shear stress exposure and total work output |
| Advanced aerobic | 1–2 HIIT sessions/week (4×4 min at 85–95% HRmax, or 10×1 min sprints) | Maximize VO₂max, FMD, and mitochondrial stimulation; highest time efficiency |
| Resistance | 2–3 sessions/week, 6–8 exercises per session, 8–12 RM × 3 sets | Progressive overload; supplement 25–30 g protein within 2 hours post-exercise |
| Flexibility/balance | 3 sessions/week yoga/tai chi/static stretching (mandatory for 65+) | Fall prevention, joint function |
| Individualized adjustment | FITT graded by fitness level; sedentary individuals start at 50–60% max capacity, increase 10% weekly | Non-responders (~half of sedentary individuals with limited FMD response) need higher doses or mode switching |

**FITT individualization points**: ① Age—65+ prioritize balance and resistance, control fall risk; ② Underlying disease—coronary artery disease/heart failure patients can do HIIT after cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET) assessment (Wisløff protocol well-evidenced), hypertensive patients should avoid Valsalva straining; ③ Fitness assessment—use talk test (able to speak complete sentences during exercise ≈ moderate intensity) or heart rate reserve (Karvonen formula) for starting intensity; ④ Time allocation—separate HIIT and resistance on different days to minimize interference; ⑤ Adherence—"the best prescription is the one the patient can adhere to"; any activity (stairs, gardening) has dose-response benefits.

## 9. Exercise Safety and Cardiovascular Screening

- **Pre-exercise screening**: Sedentary individuals, those over 40 with ≥1 cardiovascular risk factor, should undergo medical evaluation (exercise stress test) before starting moderate-high intensity exercise; those with known cardiovascular disease should have cardiac rehabilitation training prescribed by a physician;
- **Acute risks**: Exercise-induced sudden cardiac death/myocardial infarction annual incidence ~1/100,000–1/50,000, mostly in undiagnosed coronary artery disease during vigorous exercise; regular exercisers have overall lower risk than sedentary individuals (exercise's cardiovascular benefits far outweigh risks);
- **Injury prevention**: Gradual progression (weekly increment ≤10%), warm up 5–10 minutes, runners should manage training load (overuse injuries correlate with sudden volume increases);
- **Special populations**: Osteoporosis patients avoid high-impact activities; diabetes patients monitor blood glucose (before/after exercise); joint degeneration patients substitute swimming/cycling for running;
- **Warning signs**: Chest pain, pre-syncope, abnormal shortness of breath during exercise—stop immediately and seek medical attention.

## 10. Open Questions and Controversies

1. **"Non-responder" problem**: ~half of sedentary healthy individuals show limited cardiovascular endpoint (FMD, VO₂max) response to short-term exercise—genetic background (gene-dose interaction) and "low responder" countermeasures (increase dose? switch mode? drug combination?) are the core unresolved questions in exercise genomics (HERITAGE Family Study);
2. **Long-term effects of extreme doses**: The right side of the U-shaped curve—the net effect of long-term extreme endurance exercise on the heart (myocardial fibrosis, atrial fibrillation risk↑) coexists with positive results from "long-lived athlete" cohorts; which predominates remains undetermined;
3. **Antioxidant controversy in mitohormesis**: Post-Ristow 2009 replication studies are inconsistent; whether antioxidant supplementation's blunting of training adaptation universally holds in elderly populations and different training modalities remains to be verified;
4. **Exercise-drug interaction timing**: Exercise + metformin (may blunt exercise adaptation), exercise + beta-blockers (heart rate-based prescriptionbecomes ineffective requires switching to RPE), exercise + NAD⁺ precursor additive/antagonistic directions all lack high-quality RCTs;
5. **Reproducibility of epigenetic age reversal**: Exercise's effects on methylation clocks are inconsistent across studies, lacking long-term randomized controlled validation;
6. **"Exercise pill" (exercise mimetics)**: GW501516 (PPARδ agonist), AICAR (AMPK agonist), and other compounds attempting to simulate endurance adaptation are effective in animals but cannot replicate exercise's systemic network effects, and GW501516 was terminated in human development due to carcinogenicity—suggesting that "holistic intervention" is difficult to replace with a single molecule.
