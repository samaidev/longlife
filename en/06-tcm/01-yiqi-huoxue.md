# Boosting Qi and Activating Blood, and the NO Pathway: A Molecular-Biological Annotation of the TCM Qi–Blood Theory

> **Central view:** “Boosting qi and activating blood” is a representative traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) treatment principle for cardiovascular and cerebrovascular aging and ischemic disease. At the molecular level, its core pathomechanism—“qi deficiency and blood stasis”—closely parallels the modern vascular-biology axis of **reduced eNOS activity → lower NO bioavailability → endothelial dysfunction → microcirculatory impairment/prothrombotic state**. Qi-tonifying herbs (astragalus, ginseng) restore the capacity to “produce” NO, while blood-activating and stasis-resolving herbs (danshen, sanqi, chuanxiong) protect NO’s “survival and effects.” In concise modern terms: “boosting qi restores eNOS activity; activating blood protects NO bioavailability”—a molecular interpretation of the traditional principle.

---

## 1. Core Concept

**Yiqi Huoxue (boosting qi and activating blood)** is a formula-based treatment principle in which qi-tonifying herbs are primary and blood-activating/stasis-resolving herbs secondary, or vice versa. It is used for the pattern of qi deficiency with blood stasis: weakened “qi” fails to propel “blood,” slowing circulation and obstructing the channels. Its importance has three foundations: ① theoretical—the qi-deficiency/blood-stasis pattern is common in cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease in middle-aged and older adults (coronary heart disease, stroke, hypertension) and in diabetic vascular complications; ② clinical—formulas such as Buyang Huanwu Decoction, Tongxinluo, and Compound Danshen Dripping Pills are among the most widely used TCM categories for cardiovascular and cerebrovascular prevention and treatment in China; ③ scientific—the pharmacological core of this principle overlaps substantially with NO bioavailability, a key quantitative indicator of vascular aging (see 01-foundations/03-no-basics.md and 02-vascular/01-no-cardiovascular.md), giving the TCM qi–blood theory a testable molecular anchor. **In one sentence:** Yiqi Huoxue reconstructs “vigorous qi promotes blood flow” in molecular terms—restoring the synthetic capacity of the eNOS-NO axis (boosting qi) while limiting NO clearance and damage (activating blood).

## 2. TCM Theoretical Background: Pathomechanism of Qi Deficiency with Blood Stasis

### 2.1 The qi–blood relationship: “Qi commands blood; blood is the mother of qi”

Two central propositions in TCM qi–blood theory form the basis of the qi-deficiency/blood-stasis pathomechanism:

- **Qi commands blood**: Qi propels blood, contains it within vessels, warms it, and generates it. Qi deficiency weakens propulsion, slowing blood flow and causing stasis in the channels—the pattern of “qi deficiency with blood stasis.” Failure to contain blood may also let it escape the vessels and form stasis.
- **Blood is the mother of qi**: Blood carries qi and is a source of its generation. Stasis obstructing the channels impedes qi movement and prevents new blood from forming; “blood stasis and qi stagnation” in turn worsen qi deficiency.

This creates a vicious cycle: **qi deficiency → blood stasis → further qi deficiency**. Its positive-feedback structure is topologically analogous to the vascular-biology cycle of “NO deficiency → endothelial dysfunction → oxidative stress → further NO inactivation” (see section 5 of 02-vascular/01-no-cardiovascular.md).

### 2.2 Qi deficiency with blood stasis, aging, and diseases of older age

Aging is the most prominent epidemiological feature of the qi-deficiency/blood-stasis pattern. In older adults, “upright qi” naturally declines (functions of the five zang organs deteriorate), while atherosclerosis, hemodynamic changes, and chronic low-grade inflammation accumulate. Qi deficiency with blood stasis therefore forms a shared pattern background for cardiovascular/cerebrovascular disease, diabetic complications, and cognitive decline. Clinical observations find this pattern among the most prevalent in coronary heart disease, recovery after stroke, and diabetic peripheral neuropathy. From a modern perspective, this is a **pattern-based projection of “endothelial dysfunction” and “chronic inflammation” (expanded hallmarks in López-Otín 2023)**.

## 3. Full Molecular Mechanistic Chain: Mapping from Pattern to Molecule

### 3.1 Summary table of molecular pattern mapping

| TCM pattern element | Traditional description | Modern molecular correlate |
|---|---|---|
| Qi deficiency (weak propulsion) | Fatigue, shortness of breath, reluctance to speak, weak pulse | Reduced energy-metabolic reserve (mitochondrial function↓, ATP↓, AMPK signaling blunted), reduced cardiac contractility, eNOS activity↓, NO synthesis↓, immune function↓ |
| Blood stasis (sluggish circulation) | Dusky/purple tongue or ecchymoses, fixed stabbing pain, choppy pulse | Abnormal hemorheology (whole-blood viscosity↑, erythrocyte aggregation↑ and deformability↓), microcirculatory impairment, platelet activation (P-selectin↑, TXA₂↑), endothelial dysfunction (FMD↓), fibrinogen↑, PAI-1↑, NF-κB inflammatory signaling activated |
| Combined qi deficiency and blood stasis | Fatigue + stabbing pain + dusky/purple tongue | NO deficiency + endothelial dysfunction + chronic low-grade inflammation + prothrombotic state + microcirculatory impairment |

### 3.2 Modern scientific mapping of “qi”: system functional reserve

“Qi” is a highly multifaceted concept in TCM; its modern correlate is not a single molecule but a multidimensional set of **system functional reserves**:

| Function of “qi” | Modern correlate |
|---|---|
| Propulsion | Hemodynamics (cardiac output, vascular tone), gastrointestinal motility |
| Warming | Thermogenesis and energy metabolism: mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, ATP synthesis, brown-fat thermogenesis |
| Defense | Innate/adaptive immunity: macrophage, NK-cell, T- and B-cell function |
| Containment | Vascular-barrier integrity, coagulation–fibrinolysis balance, fluid homeostasis |

**Qi deficiency = reduced functional reserve**: mitochondrial dysfunction (complex I/IV activity↓) → ATP and NAD+↓ → blunted energy-sensitive AMPK/Akt signaling → inadequate eNOS-Ser1177 phosphorylation → reduced NO-synthesis reserve. This gives an energetic annotation to “qi commands blood”: **qi (energy metabolism) is the first force driving blood flow, and NO is the effector that converts chemical energy into vasodilation**.

### 3.3 Modern scientific mapping of “blood stasis”: multilevel pathology driven by NO deficiency

The molecular basis of blood stasis can be summarized as a four-stage cascade triggered by reduced NO bioavailability:

```text
Qi deficiency (energy reserve↓/oxidative stress↑)
   │
   ▼
eNOS activity↓ (Ser1177 phosphorylation↓, Thr495 phosphorylation↑, competitive inhibition by ADMA↑)
   │  + BH4 oxidation → eNOS uncoupling → produces O₂⁻ rather than NO
   ▼
NO bioavailability↓↓ (synthesis↓ + inactivation↑: O₂⁻ quenches NO → ONOO⁻)
   │
   ├─→ sGC/cGMP/PKG↓ → vasodilatory reserve↓ → vascular resistance↑, perfusion↓
   ├─→ platelet cGMP↓ → αIIbβ3 activation↑, P-selectin exposure↑ → microthrombi
   ├─→ release of NF-κB inhibition → VCAM-1/ICAM-1↑ → leukocyte adhesion → vascular inflammation
   └─→ endothelial-barrier disruption → permeability↑ → plasma leakage → microcirculatory stasis (“stasis”)
   ▼
Clinical phenotypes: hypertension, myocardial ischemia, stroke, diabetic microvascular disease, cognitive decline
```

Key quantitative anchors: ADMA (asymmetric dimethylarginine) is an endogenous competitive eNOS inhibitor. Normal plasma levels are about 0.4–0.6 µmol/L, rising 2–3-fold in diabetes, kidney disease, and hyperhomocysteinemia. Brachial-artery FMD declines by about 1–2 percentage points per decade (about 8–10% at age 30 and 4–5% at age 70). **The degree of “blood stasis” can be read out using FMD, NOx, ADMA, and other measures—this is a modern interface for objectifying pattern differentiation.**

### 3.4 Mapping treatment principles to molecules: division and synergy of boosting qi and activating blood

- **Boosting qi (qi-tonifying herbs) = restoring NO synthesis (upstream)**: Astragaloside IV activates **Ca²⁺/PI3K/Akt → eNOS-Ser1177 phosphorylation**, promoting NO production (blocked by the NOS inhibitor L-NMMA in HUVEC studies, confirming eNOS involvement). Astragalus polysaccharides activate **AMPK** and increase PGC-1α to promote mitochondrial biogenesis. Ginsenosides Rg1/Rb1 increase NO through PI3K/Akt/eNOS; Rg1 also induces autophagy through **AMPK/mTOR**, delaying brain aging.
- **Activating blood (blood-activating/stasis-resolving herbs) = protecting NO bioavailability and directly regulating vessels (midstream/downstream)**: Tanshinone IIA and salvianolic acid B upregulate eNOS and potently scavenge O₂⁻ (reducing oxidative inactivation of NO) and inhibit NF-κB; ligustrazine antagonizes calcium channels and inhibits TXA₂; total sanqi saponins raise PGI₂, lower TXA₂ (shifting the PGI₂/TXA₂ ratio toward antithrombosis), and downregulate P-selectin and NF-κB.
- **Combining qi tonification and blood activation = “restore supply + increase activity + reduce clearance”**: Qi-tonifying herbs restore eNOS synthetic capacity; blood-activating herbs protect the NO already produced and block downstream prothrombotic/pro-inflammatory amplification. This parallels the modern vascular-pharmacology strategy of restoring eNOS function (exercise, statins, ACE inhibitors) + reducing NO clearance (antioxidants) + antiplatelet therapy (aspirin). Traditional formulation is an empirical precursor to this combined strategy.

## 5. Representative Yiqi Huoxue Formula: Buyang Huanwu Decoction

### 5.1 Composition and pathomechanism

Buyang Huanwu Decoction comes from Wang Qingren’s Qing-dynasty text *Yilin Gaicuo* and is the **leading formula for qi deficiency with blood stasis**, traditionally used for post-stroke sequelae (hemiplegia, facial deviation, impaired speech, frequent urination). Its composition is distinctive:

| Herb | Original dose | Role | Molecular correlate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Astragalus | 4 liang (about 120 g; chief herb, by far the largest dose) | Strongly tonifies primordial qi; vigorous qi promotes blood flow | Astragalosides/polysaccharides → PI3K/Akt/eNOS, AMPK → NO-synthesis reserve↑ |
| Tail of Chinese angelica root | 2 qian | Activates and nourishes blood | Ferulic acid, antiplatelet |
| Red peony root | 1.5 qian | Cools blood and activates it | Paeoniflorin, antioxidant/anticoagulant |
| Earthworm | 1 qian | Unblocks channels | Fibrinolytic activity resembling lumbrokinase |
| Chuanxiong | 1 qian | Moves qi and activates blood | Ligustrazine → NO release, calcium antagonism |
| Peach kernel, safflower | 1 qian each | Breaks stasis and unblocks channels | Antiplatelet, anti-inflammatory |

**The large astragalus dose is the formula’s soul**: qi tonification (restoring eNOS synthesis) is primary and blood activation is secondary (protecting NO and resolving established stasis), directly expressing “qi commands blood”—when qi is sufficient, stasis resolves.

### 5.2 Modern research on Buyang Huanwu Decoction and NO

- **Animal studies**: In focal cerebral ischemia (MCAO) models, Buyang Huanwu Decoction increases eNOS expression in brain and vascular tissues, raises serum NO, suppresses inflammation (TNF-α, IL-6↓) and apoptosis in ischemic regions, and promotes angiogenesis and neurological recovery. A systematic review/meta-analysis of animal studies (Wei RL et al., *eCAM*, 2013) found significant reduction in infarct volume and improved neurological scores.
- **Clinical studies**: A systematic review/meta-analysis of 19 RCTs (Hao CZ et al., 2012) found improved NIHSS neurological-deficit scores and overall response rates in acute ischemic stroke, with good safety. Evidence for use during recovery alongside standard Western care (antiplatelets, statins, rehabilitation) further supports secondary prevention and functional recovery.
- **Extended use**: Modern clinical practice has extended the formula to coronary angina, diabetic peripheral neuropathy, and vascular dementia in patients with the qi-deficiency/blood-stasis pattern, with mechanisms generally pointing to endothelial repair and improved microcirculation.

### 5.3 Evidence levels for other representative formulas (cross-reference)

Evidence for Yiqi Huoxue formulas follows a clear gradient (see 06-tcm/03-formulas.md): Shengmai Yin (small RCTs, NO↑/FMD↑) → Compound Danshen Dripping Pills (FDA international multicenter phase III for chronic stable angina) → Shexiang Baoxin Pills (MUST study) → **Tongxinluo CTS-AMI trial (Yang Y et al., *JAMA*, 2023)**: 3,797 STEMI patients presenting within 24 hours at 124 centers in China, randomized double-blind placebo-controlled. Tongxinluo significantly reduced 30-day major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events and lowered cardiac-death risk. This is the highest-level evidence for TCM in myocardial infarction; its formulation (ginseng to tonify qi + leeches, scorpions, and other animal ingredients to dredge collaterals) represents Yiqi Huoxue in severe disease.

## 6. Evidence Grading

| Level | Content | Evidence grade | Representative reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanistic studies | Astragaloside, ginsenosides, tanshinones, etc. promote NO through PI3K/Akt/eNOS | Strong (consistent in vitro/animal; blocked by L-NMMA) | Zhang SY 2022; Leung & Ng 2013 |
| Whole-animal | Buyang Huanwu Decoction/Tongxinluo upregulates eNOS and reduces infarct volume | Strong (supported by animal meta-analysis) | Wei RL 2013 (*eCAM*) |
| Human surrogate endpoints | Formulas increase serum NO, lower ET-1, and improve FMD | Moderate (mostly small RCTs, substantial heterogeneity) | Multiple Chinese RCTs |
| Human hard endpoints | Tongxinluo reduces 30-day STEMI MACCE | Moderate–high (one large double-blind RCT; lacks international replication) | Yang Y 2023 (*JAMA*) |
| Pattern-to-molecule mapping | Qi deficiency with blood stasis ↔ endothelial dysfunction/NO deficiency framework | Theoretical framework (mechanistic inference) | Multiple reviews |

**Evidence-grading point**: Mechanistic evidence for individual constituents is generally stronger than human evidence for formulas. The largest gap in TCM NO research is not whether eNOS can be activated (in-vitro/animal evidence is ample), but **reproducibility in humans using standardized preparations and hard endpoints**.

## 7. Clinical and Experimental Evidence in Detail

- **Astragaloside IV (AS-IV)**: In human umbilical-vein endothelial cells (HUVECs), AS-IV suppresses angiotensin-II-induced inflammation through the Ca²⁺/PI3K/Akt/eNOS/NO pathway; the effect disappears after adding the eNOS inhibitor L-NMMA, showing dependence on NO production. In streptozotocin (STZ)-induced diabetic rats, AS-IV increases eNOS and improves endothelium-dependent relaxation (Zhang SY et al., *China Journal of Chinese Materia Medica*, 2022, PMID 36472009).
- **Ginsenoside Rg1**: In D-galactose aging models, it regulates autophagy through AMPK/mTOR and delays brain aging (Zhang Jin et al., *Chinese Pharmacological Bulletin*); in a yeast chronological-aging model it extends lifespan, strengthens stress resistance, and lowers ROS (PMID 36829855). Rg3 increases skeletal-muscle mitochondrial DNA copy number through SIRT1 activation—ginsenosides’ “qi-tonifying anti-aging” effects have cross-species evidence.
- **Total sanqi saponins (PNS)**: Classic pharmacology shows PNS raises carotid PGI₂ and lowers platelet TXA₂ (Shi L et al., *Acta Pharmacologica Sinica*, 1990;11(1):29–32), shifting the prostaglandin/thromboxane ratio toward anticoagulation and providing a molecular explanation for “resolving stasis while stopping bleeding; activating blood without damaging it.”
- **Buyang Huanwu Decoction**: A meta-analysis of 19 RCTs (Hao CZ et al., 2012) found improved neurological-deficit scores in ischemic stroke; animal meta-analysis (Wei RL et al., *eCAM*, 2013) found significant reduction in infarct volume.
- **Tongxinluo CTS-AMI**: A double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 3,797 STEMI patients at 124 centers found a significant reduction in 30-day MACCE (Yang Y et al., *JAMA*, 2023;330(16):1534–1545).
- **Human surrogate-endpoint pattern**: Multiple Yiqi Huoxue formulas in coronary disease consistently report increased serum NO, reduced ET-1, and improved FMD, typically in samples of 50–300 patients treated for 4–24 weeks.

## 8. Connections to the NO/NAD+/Longevity Network

Yiqi Huoxue occupies a hub position in the longevity-signaling network and intersects with three major themes:

1. **Bidirectional SIRT1↔eNOS activation**: SIRT1 deacetylates eNOS (Lys496/Lys506), directly enhancing activity (Mattagajasingh I et al., *PNAS*, 2007), while NO feeds back to enhance SIRT1 activity. The “NAD+/SIRT1 network” and “NO pathway” amplify one another. Astragaloside and ginsenosides may activate both axes through AMPK (AMPK phosphorylates eNOS and upregulates NAD+ metabolism).
2. **AMPK↔NO positive feedback**: AMPK phosphorylation of eNOS-Ser1177 promotes NO; NO in turn activates AMPK—positive feedback between metabolic and vascular health. **“Qi” (energy metabolism/AMPK) and “blood” (NO/blood flow) meet end-to-end in this molecular loop**: qi-tonifying herbs activate AMPK/eNOS and thus improve both energy metabolism and endothelial function.
3. **Mitochondrial biogenesis**: NO→cGMP/PKG→PGC-1α upregulation and astragalus polysaccharides→AMPK→PGC-1α expand mitochondrial capacity through two routes. NO can also reversibly inhibit complex IV, reducing electron leakage (a “throttle”). This aligns with the mapping of “warming” to thermogenic metabolism.

**Distinctive interpretation of this knowledge base**: Yiqi Huoxue can be viewed as a traditional version of endogenous AMPK/SIRT1/eNOS triple-axis activation, converging at the same “NO bioavailability” output as exercise, caloric restriction, and dietary nitrate (see 01-foundations/03-no-basics.md). TCM’s distinctive value is its multi-component formula action on synthesis (qi tonification), survival (blood activation/antioxidant effects), and function (antiplatelet effects), covering combinations of targets that are difficult for a single Western drug to address.

**Position of Yiqi Huoxue as an “anti-aging prescription”**: In the layered framework of [09-immortality/04-roadmap](../09-immortality/04-roadmap.md), Yiqi Huoxue belongs to “phase 1 (0–5 years, interventions ready now)” as a TCM adjunct. It does not aim to reverse aging; it restores NO bioavailability and improves microcirculation and metabolism, building a vascular-metabolic foundation for more advanced interventions (reprogramming, senolytics). **Potential population**: middle-aged/older adults with fatigue, shortness of breath, poor microcirculation (cold hands/feet, purple/dusky tongue), low FMD, and declining exercise tolerance. **Potentially unsuitable**: excess-heat or yin-deficiency with effulgent fire patterns, bleeding tendency, or concurrent potent anticoagulation. Combining with dietary nitrate (dark leafy greens) and exercise theoretically creates synergistic “threefold NO-pathway activation” (herbal formula + nitrate bypass + shear stress), but direct combination studies are lacking—an opportunity for prospective research.

## 9. Intervention Directions and Practical Recommendations

- **Pattern differentiation first**: Yiqi Huoxue is intended for people with clear signs of “qi deficiency with blood stasis” (fatigue, shortness of breath plus a dusky/purple tongue or ecchymoses and fixed stabbing pain). **People with yin deficiency and effulgent fire or excess-heat patterns should not use warming tonics**; assessment should be performed by a qualified TCM practitioner. Under medical guidance, consider evidence-supported formulas such as Buyang Huanwu Decoction, Tongxinluo, Compound Danshen Dripping Pills, or Shexiang Baoxin Pills; do not self-prescribe excessive doses or prolonged use.
- **Dose and combination**: A large astragalus dose (30–120 g in Buyang Huanwu Decoction) is key to “qi tonification as the principal action.” Start blood-activating herbs (leeches, animal-derived ingredients, danshen) at lower doses and adjust the qi-tonifying/blood-activating ratio over time.
- **Add lifestyle interventions**: Aerobic exercise (shear stress→eNOS, ≥150 minutes/week of moderate-to-vigorous activity) and dietary nitrate (dark leafy greens, beetroot; avoid excessive mouthwash use that disrupts oral microbiota) are the most reliable foundations for improving NO bioavailability. They complement rather than replace herbal mechanisms.
- **Monitoring**: In addition to blood pressure, lipids, and glucose, periodically assess FMD, cfPWV, and NOx/ADMA as objective readouts of pattern improvement.
- **Safety boundaries**: Combining blood-activating/stasis-resolving herbs with anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs increases bleeding risk (especially animal-derived ingredients and high-dose sanqi). Excessive ginseng may cause “ginseng-abuse syndrome” (hypertension, insomnia); use rich, cloying substances cautiously in people with weak digestion.

### 9.1 Intersections with immune and metabolic aging

Yiqi Huoxue affects not only blood vessels but also intersects deeply with immune and metabolic aging:

- **Immune regulation**: Astragalus polysaccharides enhance macrophage phagocytosis and NK activity through TLR4 signaling (an immunological basis for “boosting qi and securing the exterior”). Ginsenosides regulate T-cell subsets (promoting Tregs and suppressing excessive inflammation). Many Yiqi Huoxue constituents also inhibit NF-κB (reported for tanshinones and astragaloside), showing bidirectional regulation—enhancing defense while suppressing excessive inflammation. This aligns with the “taming NO” logic in [01-no-immune](../03-immunity/01-no-immune.md) and addresses immunosenescence’s dual defects of weak defense and high inflammation (see [04-immunosenescence](../03-immunity/04-immunosenescence.md)).
- **Metabolic synergy**: Ginsenosides Rb1/Rg1 improve insulin sensitivity through AMPK activation, astragaloside improves mitochondrial function through PGC-1α, and tanshinones regulate lipid metabolism. These metabolic actions align with “recalibrating nutrient sensing” in [03-metabolic-aging](../02-vascular/03-metabolic-aging.md) and may be an adjunctive metabolic anti-aging strategy.
- **Complementarity with Western anti-aging drugs**: Yiqi Huoxue (multi-component, multi-target, relatively gentle), metformin (AMPK target), rapamycin (mTOR inhibition), and senolytics (clearance) partly overlap molecularly but have different emphases. **TCM is positioned not as a replacement but as “formula-based complementarity”**: adding a pattern-matched formula to evidence-based Western drugs could theoretically cover targets not addressed by the latter, such as multiple NO-pathway nodes. This hypothesis needs “TCM + Western drug” combination RCTs; current evidence is limited to scattered small studies.

## 10. Open Questions and Controversies

1. **Difficult attribution in multi-target formulas**: Yiqi Huoxue formulas contain dozens of compounds. Serum pharmacology and metabolite studies have not yet clarified which active molecules drive the effect or how they interact. “Multi-target” is both an advantage and an obstacle to attribution.
2. **Quality control**: Differences in herb origin, processing, and extraction cause batch-to-batch variation in astragaloside, tanshinone, and ginsenoside content, limiting reproducibility and international regulatory acceptance. Pesticide residues and heavy metals require continued monitoring.
3. **Molecular diagnosis of patterns**: Quantitative diagnostic criteria for qi deficiency with blood stasis using ADMA, NOx, FMD, etc.—and precise matching of formula, pattern, and biomarker—remain undeveloped.
4. **Dose–response relationship**: Systematic research is lacking on optimizing the ratio of qi tonification to blood activation, treatment duration, and discontinuation.
5. **Replication of hard endpoints**: CTS-AMI is a single (albeit large) double-blind RCT; independent international multicenter replication is needed. Most surrogate-endpoint studies are small and at risk of positive-publication bias.
6. **Interactions with Western drugs**: High-quality data are lacking on bleeding risk with aspirin, P2Y12 inhibitors, or DOACs and on CYP450-mediated drug interactions.
